## Findings, ranked

### 1. High: the final-state rule makes a valid step off the found route cost 11–13 errors, so blocks will shorten

**Where:** `grpo_groups.judged_in_block`. UNKNOWN now updates the reference state, and the gain is
`reward.parts - state`.

**What changed.** A later tactic reaching an unknown state now gets R(unknown) − R(previous):
- after one proof-graph step that is −1.125;
- after two steps it is −1.325.

On master it was untrained, and the score kept the proof-graph prefix.

**How common this is.** It is the normal case for multi-tactic blocks, not an edge case:
- an intermediate state that no node has is UNKNOWN by construction (`judge`: `node_id` is None, so there is no
  evidence);
- every intermediate state of a block that does not itself reach the proof graph is therefore unknown, unless it
  matches a proof-graph key;
- with blocks of 4–5 flattened tactics, any sample that shares its first `have` or first step with the found proof and
  then diverges takes the full drop at the divergence;
- in the DESIGN.md example, samples B and C are of this kind.

**Stop-versus-continue arithmetic** (`rl/README.md`):
- Continuing after a proof-graph state is worth δ·P(on route) − c·P(error) − 1.125·P(valid, unknown) − drop·P(explored
  off graph).
- When the alternative to staying on the route is a valid unknown step, continuing pays only when P(on route) > 1.125 /
  1.325 = **0.85**. The design's threshold was 1/3.
- With P(on route) 0.5, P(error) 0.3 and P(unknown) 0.2, continuing is worth **−0.155** on the branch. It was +0.07 on
  master and is +0.05 under the proposal below. So the policy should stop.

**Three side effects:**
1. **Errors become cheap continuations.** Given that a block continues, an error (−0.1) costs 11× less than a valid
   alternative (−1.125). Mass among continuations shifts from valid alternatives towards errors and early stops.
2. **One-shot closers are favoured over multi-step nested proofs.** Take a claim on the found route: a failing
   one-tactic proof costs −0.1, a failing two-tactic proof costs −1.2. This also inflates the nlinarith metric in the
   watch list, for the wrong reason.
3. **Explanations are penalised for progress.** The explanation gets score − b, and B's score falls from 1.25 to
   −0.075. Explanations that lead to ambitious blocks are pushed down. The diagnosis lists halved explanations as a
   symptom.

**Final state against best prefix (question 2).**

| Rule | B's score | Continuation advantage | Continue pays when (alternative: valid unknown) | Appending valid junk after a proof-graph prefix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| final state (branch) | −0.075 | −1.33 | P(on route) > 0.85 | heavily punished, and genuine continuations with it |
| best prefix / master (unknown skipped) | 1.25 | untrained | always | free; the search's child is the junk state |
| **proposed: unverified continuation** | 1.15 | −c = −0.10 | P(on route) > c/(c+δ) = 1/3 | costs c, like an error |

**Why neither literal rule fits.**
- The final-state rule is aligned with the search in form: the search keeps the last state, so the sample contributed
  that child, and the proof-graph prefix state exists only because another edge reached it.
- But it values that child at −0.075, below a dead end. A valid tactic from a provable state usually leads to a
  provable state; rewriting, `simp at`, `have … := proof`, `intro`, `rcases`, `norm_num` and `field_simp` all preserve
  provability. Only goal-strengthening tactics can lose it: `apply`, `refine`, `left`/`right`, `exfalso`, `use`,
  `gcongr`.
- So "unknown after a proof-graph state" is mostly "provable, unverified", not "worthless".
- Best prefix ignores what the search actually got from the sample.

**Recommendation.** An UNKNOWN state reached after a state with evidence (proof graph or off graph) does not replace
the reference:
- its tactic gets 0 when a later tactic of the block reaches a state with evidence;
- it gets −c when the block ends there;
- the sample's score is the reference's R minus the wasted costs.

This keeps:
- the explored-off-graph drop, which rests on evidence;
- the anchor's unknown score, −0.075, used when the block has no evidence state before it;
- the design's 1/3 threshold.

It is a few lines in `judged_in_block`; `proposed_rule.py` is a prototype. Prediction on the branch as it is: mean
block length falls over 2–3 phases, and the share of single-tactic blocks rises. The watch list has thresholds for
both.

### 2. High: a bare claim still beats trying to prove it and failing, the residual of the hack's own mechanism

**Where:** `judged_in_block`, together with `with_unproved_claims`. The claim's state is the same node whether or not
a nested proof follows: the search keeps the prefix.

**The mechanism.**
- `have h : A := by nlinarith […]` whose nlinarith fails gets UNPROVED_CLAIM (−0.15) for the claim, plus −c for the
  failing tactic: **−0.25**.
- A bare `have h : A` gets **−0.15**.
- On master the same −0.10 sat on the nested proof's tokens while the bare form was untrained. That is one of the two
  routes by which `have … := term/by` fell from 1,261 to 242 while bare haves rose to 10,416.
- The fix raises both forms by the same −0.15 and leaves the −0.10 difference in place.

**When attempting pays.** For a claim off the found route, success turns the claim from UNPROVED_CLAIM (−0.15) into
UNKNOWN (−0.075). Writing the proof therefore pays only when p·0.075 > (1 − p)·0.1, that is **p > 0.57**.

**Recommendation.** After a complete block whose last applied tactic is an UNPROVED_CLAIM, give the closing tokens
−c instead of 0, and the score −c more. "Stopping with an open claim" is then the wasted decision:
- bare: −0.25;
- attempted and failed: −0.25;
- attempted and succeeded: ≥ −0.075.

Writing the proof is then never worse than leaving it out (`proposed_rule.py`, rows C and D). Waiving the −c on the
failing tactic directly after a claim would also make the two forms tie, but leaves no push away from the bare form.

### 3. Medium-high: UNPROVED_CLAIM needs no evidence and covers every goal-adding tactic

**Where:** `TacticGraph.with_unproved_claims`. It relabels UNKNOWN as well as OFF_GRAPH, for any tactic that raises
the goal count.

**What it catches besides bare haves.** It catches:
- an unexpanded child (no reachable state, so the claim is trivially "unproved");
- `constructor`, `by_cases`, `rcases … with h | h`, `cases`, `induction` and `interval_cases`, which all preserve
  provability: the subgoals are provable when the parent is;
- side goals of `apply`, `refine`, `rw [div_le_iff …]` and `gcongr`.

When the search simply did not reach the child, these score like a Lean error (−0.15), against −0.075 for any other
unexplored step. That is a push away from case splits and from stated lemmas exactly when budget, not evidence,
decided (question 1: "avoiding `have` where a stated lemma is right").

**Why the claim data does not cover them.**
- The claim data (2.0% → 0.2% proved) justifies a pessimistic prior for model-invented claims.
- It says nothing about structural splits.
- The unproved-claim checks (`claims_proved`, `bare_haves`) only see `have`, so the other heads go unwatched.

**Recommendation:**
- Relabel UNKNOWN → UNPROVED_CLAIM only for model-invented propositions: `have`/`obtain … :`/`suffices`/`replace`.
- Relabel OFF_GRAPH → UNPROVED_CLAIM (explored, with evidence) for every head.
- Before deciding, measure on the phase 1–10 trees, per tactic head:
  - the share of UNPROVED_CLAIM labels whose child was never expanded;
  - the claim-proved rate.
- Extend the habit monitor to goal-adding openings by head (watch list).

### 4. Medium: explored off graph (0) is still the best non-proof outcome, and filler reaches it most easily

**Where:** the ordering, off graph 0 > unknown −0.075 > failure −0.15 (`phase.toml`, `RewardConfig`).

**Why filler gets there.**
- A filler step that adds a harmless, non-closed-form hypothesis leaves the parent's state almost unchanged, e.g.
  `have : 0 ≤ x^2 := sq_nonneg x`.
- Its value estimate is the parent's, and the parent is a proof-graph node. So A* expands it soon after the real
  progress children.
- It reaches ≥ 4 expanded nodes and becomes OFF_GRAPH at 0. That is 0.15 above a failed real attempt and 0.075 above
  any novel unexplored step (G against E and F in the table).
- So the remaining hack path is "steer towards states the search will explore", which is exactly what near-copies of
  the parent are (question 1, last point).

**Two further problems with the ordering.**
- **Duplicates beat novelty.** A sample that duplicates an explored sibling's state gets 0; a novel state gets −0.075.
  That is a pressure against diversity, and distinct samples per expansion is already a health check.
- **The evidence points the other way.** A state that was explored and not proved is less likely provable than one
  that was never looked at, so explored off graph should not score above unknown.

**Recommendation:**
- Score explored off graph like unknown (`off_graph` L = `unknown_local` = −0.5, so −0.075); dead ends optionally −1.
- The order becomes: proof graph ≫ {unknown, explored off graph} −0.075 > {failure, no progress, redundant, unproved
  claim} −0.15.
- A real attempt then beats any valid non-proof step when its success rate is above
  0.075 / (1.05 + 0.15) = **6.25%**. Against explored off graph at 0 the break-even is 12.5%; on master, against
  untrained outcomes with b = 0.12, it was 22.5%.
- Keep μ = 0 meanwhile (finding 6).

### 5. Medium: the closed-form detector is too broad where it is unneeded and trivially evaded where it matters

**Where:** `AddedHypothesis.closed_form` and `TacticGraph.judge`.

**Too broad.**
- Under a statement without local names, every addition is closed-form (the README says so). So in numeric problems,
  every off-route `have` is REDUNDANT at −0.15, including real computations
  (`have h : 2^10 % 7 = 2 := by norm_num`).
- With local names, genuine closed lemmas are hit too: `Nat.Prime 7`, `Real.pi > 3`, `(2:ℝ) ≠ 0` before `field_simp`.
- That is a push away from stated lemmas on exactly the mathd computation problems.

**Too narrow.** One mention of a context name escapes it: `0 < (1:ℝ) + 0 * x`, `0 ≤ x^2`, `x + 0 = x`. So "gaming
closed-form" costs the policy one token.

**Recommendation:**
- Do not apply it when the context has no names.
- Restrict it to the observed filler shape: comparisons of numerals and casts only, with no function application, `^`,
  `%`, `∣`, `√` or constants.
- Rely on finding 4's ordering, not the detector, for the evasion class, and monitor that class (watch list).
- Before deploying, print 50 random closed-form labels from the phase 1–10 trees to check the share of mislabels.

### 6. Medium (question 3): −0.075 is right at the anchor; a value-based score should wait

**Calibration at the anchor.**
- Every trainable group has a policy proof (`require_policy_proof`). With 8 samples,
  b ≥ (1.05 − 7 · 0.15) / 8 ≈ 0 and typically sits between 0 and 0.15. It goes down to −0.09 only when all seven other
  samples are claims with a failed proof, at −0.25.
- So an unknown sample has a negative expected advantage and loses probability mass. That closes the mechanism of the
  hack: untrained outcomes kept their mass while failures were pushed down.
- Going to −0.15 would make valid and invalid Lean equal off the proof graph. The valid-Lean rate is the collapse
  trigger, and entropy is already at 0.23, so I would not.
- The miscalibration lies elsewhere: in using −0.075 as the reference for later tactics (finding 1), and in explored
  off graph sitting above it (finding 4).

**Why a value-based score should wait.**
- In the code, μ reaches only `off_graph_value` (OFF_GRAPH) and the proof-graph floor; unknown states stay flat.
- Turning `value_share` on now raises explored off-graph states by up to 0.85 · 0.15 · q ≈ 0.13·q.
- A filler state's q is its parent's, which is high at a proof-graph node.
- So μ > 0 would strengthen finding 4's path, and on a value model whose calibration is unproven (v0 never predicted
  dead: dead-end AUC 0.47).

**If it is wanted later**, use a bounded, relative form on unknown and explored off-graph states alike:
`R = clip(−0.075 + 0.85 · μ · (q(s) − q(node)), −0.15, 0)` with **μ = 0.15**. Then:
- filler (q(s) ≈ q(node)) stays at −0.075;
- the value model can move a state by at most ±0.075, so it can never beat the proof graph or fall below an error.

**Conditions before turning it on:**
1. the value model's AUC for "becomes proof graph" on resolved unknown states is ≥ 0.7;
2. P(proof graph | unknown) has been measured on statements searched twice in a phase, by goal key across their two
   trees.

Measurement 2 is also the honest calibration check for the −0.075 itself.

### 7. Medium: the habit checks compare against phases that saw different statements, and miss the likely migrations

**Where:** `health_checks.health_thresholds`, `backfilled_habits` and `tree_summary.block_habits`.

**The reference is not comparable.** The reference is phases 1–2: v0 and v1 at curriculum band 2.5. Today 65% of the

**The likely migrations go unseen.** The checks see `have` only. They do not see:
- `obtain`, `suffices`, `constructor` or `refine` openings;
- non-closed-form one-shot filler;
- block length, the drop events of finding 1, or the ratio of `have … := proof` to bare `have` (finding 2).

**Recommendation:**
- Compute the habits on the `below_band` slice too, and compare that slice with phases 1–2.
- Add the habits per version to the probe summaries. The probe is the diagnosis' own fixed 168 problems, and the
  cleanest comparison.
- Record the last phase or phases before the fix, under the current recipe, as the restart point.
- Write a revert rule for the fix itself (in the verdict); every habit check is a warning only.

### 8. Low-medium (question 4): the KL penalty, lr and curriculum are aligned with the fix but cannot hold it

**No conflict.** The KL penalty pulls towards the starting policy (v0), whose habits are the healthy ones: more
nlinarith openings, longer explanations. It works with the fix. Nothing is redundant except two overlapping
explanation checks:
- the new `explanation_characters` (median characters, < 0.7×);
- the old `explanation_length` (mean tokens, outside 0.5–2×).

The old 0.5× bound would not have caught 143 → 89 (0.62×). Raising its lower bound to 0.7× and dropping one of the two
would be enough.

**Magnitudes.**
- The k3 gradient per token is 0.01 · (1 − π_ref/π): 0.005 for a token at twice the reference's probability.
- The advantages it competes with are 0.075–1.33.
- So the penalty damps drift on low-advantage tokens but cannot stop a reward-driven habit shift, such as finding 1's
  push to stop early.
- Do not count on it to cover a reward defect.

**More gradient per phase.**
- Unknown tactics were about 20% of group tactics on the old trees, and they are now trained.
- The drops of finding 1 add new advantages of −1.1 to −1.3.
- At the same lr 2e-5 a phase therefore moves the policy more than before. Expect the negative-advantage clip fraction
  (`SignedRatios`) to rise.

**Confounding.** Four changes landed within a day: KL, lr, curriculum and reward. The phases already run under the
new recipe with the old reward are the only control, so mark the deployment phase. With lr 2e-5 and the KL, expect
habits to recover over 3–5 phases, not in one.

**The curriculum.** Below-band statements are solved early, so most siblings stay unknown. There:
- the 0.075 gap is small next to b;
- but finding 1's drop hits every block that starts like the proof and diverges.

The easy third of the curriculum is where block shortening would show first.

### 9. Low: `ClaimProofs` counts goals

**Where:** `tactic_labels.ClaimProofs`.

A reachable state with ≤ the goal count before the claim counts as "proved". That is fooled by:
- `swap`, `pick_goal` or `on_goal 2 =>` that close the main goal first;
- `induction` or `constructor` after only the first case.

The README notes this, and the risk is low while nothing rewards it. If goal-reordering tactics rise (watch list),
compare goal keys instead: the claim's goal is gone from the list.

### 10. Low: sequence credit inherits finding 1 at full strength

**Where:** `phase_sequence_credit.toml`.

Under sequence credit every token of B gets −0.10 − b, its correct prefix included. That only matters if the
single-step comparison is ever re-run with these labels.

## Verdict

**The diagnosis is right.** The branch closes the hack's main hole: untrained unknown outcomes and bare claims kept
their mass while real attempts that failed were pushed down. At the first tactic the new ordering is sound.

**Not ready to deploy as is.** Two problems should be fixed first; both are small changes in `judged_in_block` and the
closing advantage:
- **Finding 1** creates a new and strong incentive towards short blocks and early stops, and penalises progress in the
  explanation.
- **Finding 2** leaves the exact −0.10 that taught the policy to drop the proofs of its claims.

Also before deploying:
- Exempt statements without local names from the closed-form rule (finding 5).
- Replay the phase 1–10 trees offline with the final labels. This needs no GPU and is the only control available.
  Report:
  - the label shares;
  - the count of drop events and their share of the summed |advantage|;
  - UNPROVED_CLAIM by tactic head and by whether the child was expanded;
  - 50 closed-form labels;
  - the scores of bare `have` against `have` with a failed proof.

Findings 3 and 4 are recommended; they can follow once the replay has measured them. Keep μ at 0.

**Answers in short:**
1. **New hacks.** The ordering removes the incentive at the first tactic. It creates:
   - short blocks (finding 1);
   - avoidance of structural splits and stated lemmas left to the search (finding 3);
   - a preference for one-shot closers (finding 1);
   - closed-form evasion (finding 5).

   Steering towards explored off-graph states remains (finding 4).
2. **Neither rule as such.** Use an unverified continuation at −c against the last state with evidence.
3. **−0.075 is right as the anchor's score**, wrong as a later tactic's reference, and explored off graph should not
   sit above it. Use no value term for now; if one is wanted, the bounded relative form with μ = 0.15.
4. **The KL penalty, lr and curriculum are aligned, not redundant**, but too weak to cover a reward defect. The
   curriculum mix invalidates the habit reference.
5. **The watch list follows.**

**Revert rule for the fix.** By the end of phase 3, if the probe solves are not above the pre-fix level and either
bare haves have not fallen or mean block length is down more than 25%, then:
- go back to the last pre-fix version;
- apply findings 1 and 2 before retrying.
