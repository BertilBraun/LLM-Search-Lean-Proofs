## Findings, by severity

### 1. (High) The A/B has no decision rule, and 2 phases resolve only large effects

**Where.** `DESIGN.md` (owner decision 2026-10-30), `rl/README.md` ("Search options A/B in the loop"),
`budget_signals.arm_outcome` / `rate_difference`, the `Search options A/B of phase <k>` line in loop.log.

**Problem.**
- The docs say "2 phases, judged on solves per generated token and trainable groups". They give no threshold, no
  pooled estimate across phases, and no rule for what an inconclusive result means.
- Only the solve rates get an interval. Trainable groups, one of the two named criteria, are reported as a count and
  per tree only: no per-token rate and no interval.
- The interval is per phase. Two phases need a pooled estimate that the code does not compute. An operator reading
  two per-phase lines will either eyeball them or average them.
- A per-phase interval in every loop.log line invites peeking: stopping at the first phase whose interval excludes 0.
- **Power (delta method, ratio of sums).** For a solve rate p ≈ 0.4 (the band targets 0.3-0.5), tokens per tree with
  CV ≈ 0.35 and correlation ≈ −0.4 with solving, the per-tree variance of the log rate is about
  (1−p)/p + CV² − 2ρ·CV·√((1−p)/p) ≈ 2.0. The SE of the log ratio between two arms of n trees each is then about
  2/√n. Binomial noise in "solved" dominates (1.5 of the 2.0), so better cost measurement does not shrink it.

  | trees per arm | phases (~400 per arm per phase) | 95% half-width of the ratio | smallest effect found at 80% power |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 400 | 1 | ±20% | 28% |
  | 800 | 2 | ±14% | 20% |
  | 1,600 | 4 | ±10% | 14% |
  | 2,400 | 6 | ±8% | 11% |
  | 3,200 | 8 | ±7% | 10% |

  Plausible effects of a 4-sample feedback round on solves per token are around −10% to +10%. Its extra tokens
  partly cancel its extra solves. Two phases will very likely end inconclusive. Check the assumption after phase 1:
  pooled over k phases, the half-width is about the phase-1 interval's half-width divided by √k.

**Recommendation.**
- Adopt the decision rule at the end of this review before the A/B starts, and write it into DESIGN.md.
- Add an offline report (a small script over `trees/index.jsonl`) that pools all A/B phases with a stratified
  bootstrap (resample trees within phase × arm, 10,000 draws). Have it report solves per 1k tokens (all tokens and up
  to the first proof), trainable groups per 1k tokens, and active tree-hours, each as a ratio with its interval.
- Plan for 4-6 phases, with looks at 2, 4 and 6. Running longer is cheap: if the option is 10% worse, 6 phases at
  half exposure cost about 0.3 phase of efficiency.
- **Optional, about 2.5× the power.** Pair the design: feed a share of statements to both arms, and bootstrap by
  statement. Solving is strongly statement-dependent, so outcomes of the same statement correlate highly (ρ ≈ 0.6
  would cut the variance of the difference by 0.4×). The costs are half as many distinct statements consumed per
  paired tree, and two trees of one statement in a phase's training data.

### 2. (Medium-High) After a rollback, samples from the collapsed version train the next phase, from the rolled-back adapter

**Where.** `loop.py` `start_held_out_states` (resume on `generation_finished`), `finish_phase` → `check_health` →
`roll_back` (`publish_rollback` runs on the CPU *before* `pause_searches`), `run_phase` (`self.collecting = phase + 1`),
`policy_trainer.py` (old log-probabilities are recomputed by the training model, the sampler's only feed the
agreement diagnostics).

**Scenario.** The early resume starts sampling on version k+1 before its held-out check is in. Version k+1 serves until
the swap in `roll_back`, so its exposure window W is:
- the held-out tail (2-8 min);
- then `check_health`;
- then, on a collapse, the CPU merge of the rollback adapter (a 4B base: about 1-3 min);
- then the pause drain and the swap.

So W is about 4-12 min.
- **Trees affected.** About 30-100 trees finish inside W. All 112 in flight carry some expansions from version k+1.
  At about 2.5-3 expansions per search-minute that is roughly 1,100-4,000 expansions, about 4-15% of a phase's
  expansions. They are all in phase k+1's directory and are kept.
- **What training does with them.** The next phase trains from the rolled-back adapter on them as if on-policy. The
  ratio's "old" log-probabilities are the training model's own, so no importance weight discounts samples from a
  policy that by definition sits far from it.
  - Negatives (the collapse's invalid Lean) mostly push down text the good policy rarely writes. That is harmless.
  - Positives are the risk. In a group of 8 where one collapse-style sample succeeded, that one sample gets a large
    positive advantage, pulling the good adapter toward the collapse mode.
  - A second collapse within 4 phases is a hard stop, so this risks the run, not just a phase.
- **The hard stop path.** The same window applies. The loop stops, and after `grpo_loop.py rollback` and a restart
  those trees train in phase k+1 just as well.
- **Side effects, minor.**
  - About 30-100 statements are consumed by a broken policy and recorded as failures in the curriculum: one cohort
    can lower the band once. Statements are fed only once.
  - Phase k+1's `valid_blocks` dips, so the held-out states run at the next pause.
  - Phase k+1's A/B numbers include the collapsed version's trees.

Before this change there was no exposure at all: the searches stayed paused until the health check.

**Is it acceptable?** The early resume itself, yes. It recovers 2-8 min of four idle GPUs every 2 h (2-6% of loop
time), and a collapse should be rare. Keeping collapsed-version samples in training, no: the fix is cheap and a
rollback is exactly when the run is fragile.

**Recommendation, in order of value.**
1. **Gate the early resume on a provisional verdict.**
   - `StatesProgress` already sees every finished state. Have it also write the running valid and sample counts to
     `progress.json`.
   - Resume early only if the valid rate so far (at least ~75% of the states are finished by then) is at least
     0.8 × version 0's. That is a margin over the 0.7 collapse line, because the tail states are the slow ones and
     lean invalid.
   - Otherwise wait for the result as before. A healthy version then keeps the speed-up, and a collapsing one almost
     never samples.
2. **On a collapse, pause first, then merge.** Move `pause_searches` before `publish_rollback`. A few idle minutes on
   a rare event are cheaper than sampling the broken policy.
3. **Drop the collapsed version's expansions from training.**
   - Record the collapsed version in the rollback (the health record has it).
   - Have `training_request` pass `excluded_policy_versions`.
   - Have `policy_groups` (or `tree_policy_data`) skip groups whose `ExpansionTexts.policy_version` is excluded.
     These are exact per round, so a mixed tree keeps its good-version groups.
   - Drop trees that are mostly from the excluded version from the value window.
   - Apply the same after a hard stop and a manual `rollback`.
   - Optionally re-feed the statements whose trees were dropped.

### 3. (Medium) The early resume can run about 112 + ~100 Lean REPLs at once, and contend with the rollback signal's Lean checks

**Where.** `loop.py` `start_held_out_states` → `resume_searches()`; `work()` applies `sampling_limit()` only before
taking a *new* statement; `evaluation.paused_repl_memory_gib = 0.0` (every paused search's REPL stopped before the
held-out run); `single_step.search_states` stops idle REPLs only as tail states finish.

**Problem.**
- **REPL count.** At `generation_finished` most of the last ≤112 held-out states still hold a REPL. The resume
  releases the gate for *all* in-flight searches (up to 112), and each restarts its REPL and replays its states.
  The limit (`112 − unfinished`, at least 56) only stops workers from taking new statements. The transient peak is
  about 112 + ~100 REPLs. The probe's start peaks at about 112 + 56, which the memory budget was reasoned for. The
  README's memory paragraph says the resumed searches "fill what those leave, at least
  `sampling.min_concurrency_beside_evaluations`", which reads as a cap of 112 in total. It is not.
- **CPU contention.** 112 REPL restarts, with their Mathlib imports and replays, compete for cores with the held-out
  tail's Lean checks. If the 60 s tactic limit is wall-clock, contention turns slow-but-valid checks into timeouts,
  which count as not valid. That lowers `held_out_valid_lean`, the rollback signal. The bias is bounded, since it hits
  only tail checks near the limit, but it is one-sided and absent from version 0's reference.

**Recommendation.**
- Resume early only once the held-out job's `searches_in_flight` is at most `concurrency − min_concurrency` (≤56),
  so that the peak matches the probe's start. Combine this with finding 2's gate.
- Read the `as the searches resume beside the held-out states` host-memory line at the first such pause.
- Count the held-out wall-clock timeouts of states finished after the resume against those before it. If they
  differ, either keep the searches paused through the tail or make the held-out limit heartbeat-only.
- Fix the README sentence.

### 4. (Medium) Solves per tree-hour is biased toward feedback, and pause waits make it noisy

**Where.** `budget_signals.wall_seconds` (`TreeEntry.wall_seconds`, the search's wall clock); README: "A tree's wall
clock includes a pause it waited through, alike in both arms."

**Problem.**
- **Shared load.** The arms share the vLLM servers. The feedback arm's extra requests lengthen every request's
  queue, so control trees in the A/B run slower than control trees would without feedback, and feedback trees run
  faster than under a full rollout, where everyone sends rounds. Both biases favour feedback on any wall-clock
  metric. Tokens are load-independent, so solves per token is not biased this way.
- **Pause waits.** A tree spanning a pause gains about 10-15 min. The chance of spanning is proportional to the
  tree's length, so in expectation the inflation is proportional and the ratio of sums is roughly unbiased. That much
  of the README is right, but "alike" is not. The cost is variance: about 112 trees per phase carry +10-15 min, about
  10% of a phase's tree-hours concentrated in about 14% of the trees. The early resume adds a third pause-like wait
  (finding 2's window).
- **Slots.** Tree-hours are the right currency only when slots (REPL memory, Lean cores) bind. The loop config says
  "most searches wait on the GPUs", which makes tokens the binding cost.

**Recommendation.**
- Make tokens the primary cost and tree-hours a guardrail.
- Measure active seconds: wall seconds minus the time spent in `PauseGate` (`GatedExpander` can sum it into the
  accounting).
- Check which resource binds from vLLM's queue (requests waiting at concurrency 112) on the dashboard. If the GPUs
  are not saturated, tree-hours matter more, and the bias above has to be stated next to the result.

### 5. (Low-Medium) "Solves per generated token": all tokens are the right cost, but the split is needed to read the result

**Where.** `TreeSummary.generated_tokens` (the whole tree, harvest included) versus
`generated_tokens_to_first_proof` (recorded, summed, but no rate or interval).

**Answer.**
- Decide on **all tokens**. The loop always harvests (up to 16 expansions after the first proof). The feedback round
  also fires during the harvest, at other failing nodes, and that is real GPU time under a rollout.
- Tokens to the first proof isolate search efficiency. They differ from all tokens only in solved trees, since an
  unsolved tree's tokens are all "to the first proof".
- Report both rates with intervals. The split is diagnostic:
  - if feedback wins on tokens to the first proof but not on all tokens, the loss is rounds fired while harvesting;
  - the next variant to try is then "no feedback round once the root is proved" (one condition in
    `feedback_fires`), not dropping feedback.
- Harvest tokens buy trainable groups, not solves. That is why groups per token (finding 6) must sit beside solves
  per token.
- Prompt tokens: a feedback prompt appends 2 attempts and errors after the shared prefix, so vLLM's prefix cache
  covers most of it. `Accounting.prompt_tokens` exists, so add it per arm to confirm the prefill cost is small.

### 6. (Low-Medium) Trainable groups are compared per tree, unweighted, and without an interval

**Where.** `ArmOutcome.trainable_groups_per_tree`, `feedback_trainable_groups`.

**Problem.**
- Groups per tree ignores that feedback trees cost more tokens.
- A feedback group (4 samples, on a prompt used only in the round) counts like a first-round group of 8. Its
  transfer to the base prompt is unproven, and review M (finding 8) noted that it doubles the weight of a hard node.
- The feedback arm can also lose first-round groups: a node proved only by a feedback sample drops its first-round
  group, whose 8 failures had a signal.

**Recommendation.** Report, per arm and with an interval:
- trainable groups per 1k tokens, with feedback groups at weight 4/8 (or proof-graph nodes with any trainable group);
- first-round groups alone, per 1k tokens.

The decision rule uses the first as a non-inferiority guard.

### 7. (Low-Medium) The time-based per-phase accounting censors long feedback trees at the A/B's start and end

**Where.** `arm_outcomes` over `store.phase_entries(phase)`, which holds trees *finished* in the phase.

**Problem.**
- **In steady state** the trees finished in a window are an unbiased sample. Trees spanning a pause just count in
  the next phase.
- **At the start** (the restart that enables the A/B kills in-flight searches), fast and solved trees arrive first.
  The slower arm's unsolved trees arrive later, so its early finished set over-represents solves.
- **At the end** (the restart that disables it), the trees cut off in flight are length-biased toward the slower
  arm's unsolved ones.
- About 112 trees on each side of 1,600-4,800 is a few percent, enough to bias an effect of 5-10%.

**Recommendation.**
- Define the A/B population by start time, `finished_time − wall_seconds`: trees started in the A/B window, whenever
  they finish.
- End the A/B by switching it off only after the trees started in the window have finished. Alternatively, drop the
  first ~20 min of starts and the starts of the last ~20 min, in both arms.

### 8. (Low) Training spillover: phase 2 is not confounded between arms, but its estimand drifts

**Answer to "is phase 2 confounded?"** Not between the arms. In each phase both arms search with the same policy
versions, so the difference is still the causal effect of the round at search time, given the current policy. Two
things change, though:
- **The estimand changes across phases.** After phase 1 the policy has trained on feedback groups. Its feedback
  round gets better at a "half dose": under a rollout every tree would contribute feedback groups, about twice the
  A/B's share. So the A/B's later phases understate a rollout's round quality, and pooling averages an effect that
  drifts.
- **Changes to the first-round policy are invisible to the A/B.** Training on feedback prompts may also shift the
  first-round policy, and both arms share that. Only the pinned held-out and probe trends see it.

**Recommendation.**
- Report the difference per phase, and test the phase × arm trend in the pooled report. Pool if no trend; otherwise
  decide on the later phases.
- Add an A/A balance check to the A/B line: mean curriculum difficulty, verified share, and the root's first-round
  valid rate and distinct blocks per arm. These are the same policy and prompt on a hash-split population, so they
  must agree, and a difference would mean the split or the routing is wrong.

### 9. (Low) The health checks are only partly insulated from the A/B

**Where.** `health_checks.measurements`: `kl_to_starting_policy` and `clip_fraction` come from training over all
sequences, feedback included. The first-round tree fields are still shifted by which nodes get expanded.

**Problem.**
- **KL and clip.** Feedback sequences are on a prompt the starting policy never saw. Their KL to it and their clip
  fraction may run higher than the first round's, so the absolute thresholds (0.5 and 0.1) can fire on the A/B rather
  than on drift.
- **Node selection.** Counting first-round samples removes the round's own samples. But the feedback arm's tree
  shape changes, because feedback children are expanded later, so its later first-round samples sit at different
  nodes.

**Recommendation.**
- While the A/B runs, compute the tree-based health fields from control-arm trees only. There are still thousands of
  samples per phase.
- Log KL and clip on first-round sequences beside the totals. The trainer has `PolicySequence.feedback`.
- Update the README's paragraph on first-round signals to say which checks are covered.

### 10. (Low) Curriculum: the band moves for both arms alike, which is fair

**Answer.**
- Each statement is fed once, by a hash independent of difficulty, from the same band at the same time. So at every
  moment both arms face the same difficulty distribution, and the band's moves cancel out of the difference.
- The cohort judgement pools both arms. A feedback arm that solves more raises the band by about half its effect,
  for both arms.
- With `judged_share = 0.9`, the slower arm's trees are more often among the cohort's last 10%, which is counted
  unsolved. That lowers the band slightly for both arms, and never enters the per-arm numbers.
- What does change is the operating point. The effect is measured at the band the mixed loop chooses, a little
  harder than control alone would choose. That is second-order, unless the effect depends strongly on difficulty.
  Finding 8's balance check covers the remaining risk.

### 11. (Low) Docs and config that mislead

1. **REPL cap (README, "Host memory").** "fill what those leave, at least `min_concurrency_beside_evaluations`" reads
   as a REPL cap. All in-flight searches resume at once (finding 3). The `min_concurrency_beside_evaluations` comment
   in `loop.toml` mentions only the probe and miniF2F.
2. **Pause waits (README, A/B).** "A tree's wall clock includes a pause it waited through, alike in both arms" holds
   only in expectation, and it omits the shared-load bias (finding 4).
3. **"Search-time only" (README, A/B).** "Both arms train one policy, so the A/B measures the search-time effect
   only" is right about attribution. But from phase 2 the policy has trained on feedback groups (finding 8). Say "the
   search-time effect given the current policy".
4. **Which tokens, which groups (DESIGN and README).** "judged on solves per generated token and trainable groups"
   names neither the tokens (all, or to the first proof) nor the group normalization, and gives no rule. Replace it
   with the rule below.
5. **No end date (`loop.toml`).** The A/B has no end. "2 phases" lives only in a comment and the docs, and nothing in
   loop.log says when the planned looks are reached. Add `options_arm.until_phase` (or the first and last phase) to
   the config. The loop can then log "A/B look k due" and switch the arm off by itself, which also avoids the restart
   finding 7 censors.
6. **The rollback's window (README, `health` row).** "a held-out collapse publishes the last good version's policy
   again (searches already running pause around the swap)" omits that the CPU merge runs before that pause, so the
   collapsed version samples through it (finding 2).
7. **Unstated: what happens to the window's trees.** Nothing tells the operator that trees sampled on a collapsed
   version stay in the next phase's training data. Until finding 2 is fixed, say so in the rollback paragraph and in
   the `grpo_loop.py rollback` instructions.

## Answers in brief

**1. Early resume.**
- The mechanism is sound: `GenerationWatch` / `StatesProgress`, the stale `progress.json` removed before the job,
  the health checks awaiting the result, the restart rerunning the tail, and the sampling seconds kept across
  `pause_searches`.
- The exposure to a collapsed version is a 4-12 min window: about 30-100 finished trees and 4-15% of a phase's
  expansions. They go to phase k+1 and train it from the rolled-back adapter, without discount. The rollback keeps
  them.
- That is not acceptable as is, but cheap to fix: gate the resume on the provisional held-out rate, pause before the
  CPU merge, and drop the collapsed version's expansions from training (finding 2).
- Bound the REPL peak (finding 3).

**2. A/B fairness.**
- The curriculum is fair (finding 10).
- All tokens are the right cost, with the split to the first proof as a diagnostic (finding 5).
- Tree-hours are a biased guardrail, not a primary metric (finding 4).
- Phase 2 is not confounded between arms, but the estimand drifts (finding 8).
- Two phases detect only effects of 20% or more. Plan for 4-6 phases or a paired design (finding 1).
- Define the population by start time (finding 7).

**3. Docs.** Finding 11.

## Verdict

- **The early resume: sound in mechanism; merge it with two changes.** Gate it on the provisional held-out valid rate
  and on the job's REPLs in flight (findings 2-3). On a collapse, pause before the merge and exclude the collapsed
  version's samples from training (finding 2). Until then, the rare rollback trains the next phase on 4-15% of its
  expansions from the policy it just rejected.
- **The A/B: fair in design, with one policy, one band and a hash split.** It is not yet decidable:
  - 2 phases resolve only effects of 20% or more;
  - the second criterion (trainable groups) has no cost normalization or interval;
  - there is no rule.

  Add the pooled report and the rule below before the first look. Make tokens the primary cost; tree-hours only
  guard.
- **First-round health: correct for the sampled-output fields.** KL and clip fraction still include the feedback
  sequences. Read the tree fields from the control arm while the A/B runs.

## Decision rule (to fix before the A/B's first look)

**Population.**
- Trees whose search started after the restart that enabled the A/B (`finished_time − wall_seconds`), up to the
  current look's pause, counted whenever they finish.
- Exclude the first 20 min of starts after the restart, so both arms are in steady state.
- Exclude every expansion from a version that a held-out check judged collapsed.

**Primary.**
- R = (solves per 1k generated tokens, all tokens, feedback arm) / (the same, control arm). Each is a ratio of sums
  over trees.
- Interval: a stratified bootstrap, resampling trees within phase × arm, 10,000 draws, percentile.
- **Diagnostic, not decisive.** R₁, the same with tokens up to the first proof.

**Non-inferiority guard.**
- G = ratio of trainable groups per 1k generated tokens, with feedback groups weighted 4/8. Require the lower 95%
  bound of G ≥ 0.95.
- Guardrails:
  - If vLLM is not saturated: the active (pause-free) tree-hour ratio, lower 95% bound ≥ 0.85.
  - No held-out or health alert in the control arm's fields during the A/B that the feedback arm's would explain.

**Looks.** After A/B phases 2, 4 and 6 (about 1,600, 3,200 and 4,800 trees). Use 99% intervals at looks 2 and 4,
and 95% at look 6.
- **Adopt** at any look if the lower bound of R > 1.00, R ≥ 1.05, and the guard holds.
- **Drop as harmful** at any look if the upper bound of R < 1.00.
- **Drop for futility** at look 4 if the upper 95% bound of R < 1.05: a worthwhile gain is ruled out.
- **Drop** at look 6 if not adopted. Default to the simpler loop.
- **Inconclusive but harvest-limited.** If R is inconclusive while R₁'s lower 95% bound > 1.00, do not adopt. Start a
  new A/B with the round off once the root is proved (finding 5).

**Phase drift.** If the per-phase differences trend (phase × arm, 95%), decide on the last two phases alone, with the
same thresholds.

**Before every look.** Check the A/A balance (difficulty, verified share, root first-round valid rate and distinct
blocks). A difference outside its 99% interval stops the A/B as broken, not as a result.
