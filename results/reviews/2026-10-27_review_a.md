# Independent review: search-tree GRPO for a 4B Lean step prover

**How this review was done.** I read DESIGN.md in full, the literature and error-analysis results, and the implementation in `search/`, `rl/`, `value/`, `policy/` and `lean/`. Two kinds of claim appear below:
- **Verified**: I read the code, or ran a read-only analysis. The analyses used the project's own `label_graph`, `policy_groups` and `group_advantages`, with the settings in `phase.toml` (no_progress −0.5, shaping 0.1, λ = 0.15, no std scaling). They ran on the recorded miniF2F-valid trees of visible student v1 (n = 16, 64 expansions, 171 solved) and v2 (166 solved). - **Judgment**: anything else is marked as judgment or as from memory.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The "15% local" cap does not hold for the training step as a whole.** Within one sample's reward the local term is 15%. But 90% of all groups have only local signal (every expansion of an unsolved tree, and most off-graph expansions of solved ones). Summed over trained groups, **the local term is 87–88% of the total |advantage|** (v1 0.880, v2 0.871). It is only 21–22% inside the 172 groups that carry a global signal. So the "dense" run is mostly a policy-gradient on Lean validity.
2. **That validity signal pushes toward short, safe blocks.** About 83% of the local variation comes from error vs. no error, 14% from no-progress, and only 4% from value shaping. In local-only groups, one-line blocks get a mean advantage of +0.016 and blocks of 9+ lines get −0.021. Yet the per-sample rate of reaching the proof graph barely depends on block length. This is the exact behaviour your error analysis found to win ("longer, more ambitious blocks … yet solve more problems"). There is also an open reward-hacking route: a redundant `have h' := h₀` always creates a "new state" and scores 0 instead of −1.
3. **The global label is noisy at exactly the nodes that matter.** The search stops at the first proof. At proof-graph nodes, more valid samples lead to *never-expanded* siblings (416 in the v2 trees) than to the proof graph (276), and they all get z = 0. Their G is identical to a Lean error's. Many of them are provable. The policy is pushed to imitate whichever branch best-first happened to finish first. Since 124–126 solved trees end after one expansion, it also learns "close everything in one block".
4. **The planned μ = 0.15 value targets turn censored observations into confident negatives.** Every off-graph expanded node would get ≥ 85% "dead", whether it was searched once or exhaustively. Separately, the code reads q as `(16 − h)/15`, linear in the recorded scalar h. That is not 1 − p_dead: "far from a proof" and "unprovable" become the same thing. Trees record only h, so the probability the design asks for cannot even be computed from the records.
5. **Off-policy bookkeeping is wrong in two places.** The rollouts are sampled at temperature 0.8, but the trainer scores at temperature 1 (no logit scaling anywhere in `rl/`). And "old" log-probabilities come from the trainer's weights at the start of the phase, not from the behaviour policy. The clip and KL diagnostics therefore cannot detect behaviour/target mismatch.
6. **The outcome-only control is not a clean control.** It differs in three ways at once: no local term, sequence-level credit, and partial blocks scored 0 instead of G of their prefix (12% of proof-path edges are partial). It also has about 30× fewer gradient-carrying groups (145 vs 4,279 per 244 trees). "Dense beats control" is close to guaranteed and would not show that the dense signal is *useful*.
7. **The value gate is noise and is tuned on the evaluation split.** It accepts on +0.01 pairwise accuracy over about 50 pairs, where the standard error is about 0.065. Applied every phase, it will accept models by chance (winner's curse). The benchmark trees are miniF2F-valid, which is also the headline evaluation set.
8. **Throughput: the trainer, not Lean, will be the bottleneck** (estimate). With 8 copies of each prompt per group and 90% of groups trained, one hour of search produces on the order of 10⁵ sequences / ~10⁸ tokens. That is several hours of 4B-LoRA training on one 3090. The plan does not say what gets subsampled.
9. **Expected effect vs. noise.** Your 2B expert-iteration loop gave no gain beyond noise, and published RL stages on small provers gain a few points with 2–10× this compute (judgment). A +2–4 point gain is the same size as single-run miniF2F-valid noise (5–7 theorems). Without more sensitive evaluation, the most likely result is "no measurable improvement", even if the loop works.
10. **Things that are good and should stay:** the harness work (heartbeats, flattening with equivalence guards, `rename_i`, identifier check), proof-graph labelling with transpositions, value-free benchmark trees, Dr.-GRPO-style mean centering, and the per-token credit idea itself.

## 2. Findings

### F1. In aggregate the local term dominates the gradient; λ caps only each sample's reward — **critical** (verified)

**What I found**
- `advantages.py` mixes `(1−λ)G + λL` per sample. `policy_data.group_weight` (policy_data.py:109) gives local-only groups and global groups the same weight (`GroupWeightConfig` defaults 1.0/1.0, and `phase.toml` sets the same).
- On the v1 trees: 4,751 groups. 4,279 have mixed rewards (dense) but only 172 have a global signal.

| Measure (v1 trees) | Value |
|---|---|
| Summed \|global advantage\| | 313 |
| Summed \|local advantage\| | 2,298 |
| Local share overall (v2 trees) | 0.88 (0.871) |
| Local share inside global groups | 0.219 |

- The cause is structural. With best-first search and an early stop, an unsolved tree spends all 64 expansions, while a solved tree spends 1–3. A curriculum that balances *trees* at 50% therefore still gives ~95% of *groups* from unsolved trees. DESIGN's sentence "trees are balanced between solved and unsolved, so groups carry signal" mixes up tree balance and group balance.

**Why it matters.** The experiment's stated question is "does a dense but globally anchored signal help?". In practice this signal is not globally anchored. And the dashboard metric `local_advantage_share` will show ~0.85 on the first phase.

**Recommendation**
- Make the share hold for the training step as a whole. Either:
  - set `local_only_weight` each phase so that the summed local |advantage| is about 15–25% of the total, or
  - subsample local-only groups to a fixed fraction of the step.
- Report the local share both per step and per phase.
- Run a cheap ablation first: global-signal groups only (plus Lean-verified negatives, see F3).

### F2. The local reward is in effect a validity reward and penalizes long blocks; redundant `have` is a hack route — **major** (verified numbers; the hack is judgment)

**What I found** (local-only groups, v2 trees)
- Share of the summed |component − group mean|: failure 0.826, no_progress 0.136, shaping 0.037, goals_closed 0.001.
- Mean overall advantage by block length: 1 line +0.0164 (n = 24k); 2–8 lines −0.004 to −0.017; 9+ lines −0.021 (n = 10.7k).
- Per-sample rate of reaching the proof graph by length: 1 line 0.0066, 5 lines 0.0112, 9+ lines 0.0046. So length is not a strong predictor of success.
- The no-progress detector only catches a net-unchanged goal key. DESIGN itself notes that "an added `have` always changes the state". A block like `have h₅ := h₀`, `norm_num at h₀` (cosmetic), or `clear`/`revert`/`intro` cycles earns L ≈ 0, against −1 for a real but failing attempt. The shaping term is too small (3.7%) to counter this.

**Why it matters**
- This is the single-step "valid step" objective the project already moved past.
- It works against the planned mixed-step-length SFT.
- It invites entropy collapse onto safe tactics. Over many phases the "dense vs control" comparison may come out negative for the dense arm.

**Recommendation**
- Down-weight local-only groups (F1).
- Score "new state but no measurable progress" relative to the group, not as 0. Options:
  - L = 0 only when the value of the child improves by more than a margin over the parent, or the child is on the proof graph;
  - otherwise L slightly negative.
- Add dashboard alarms for redundant-hypothesis steps: the child state equals the parent plus hypotheses whose types already appear, or the target is unchanged.
- Track the distribution of block lengths per phase.

### F3. Never-expanded (and barely expanded) valid siblings get z = 0 in solved trees — **critical** (verified)

**What I found**
- `global_value` (grpo_groups.py:109) returns 0 for every class except PROOF_GRAPH, and DESIGN states this explicitly ("never-expanded children included").
- The search returns at the first verified proof (search.py:354, :431).
- At proof-graph-node groups, sample outcomes:

| Outcome at proof-graph nodes | v2 trees | v1 trees |
|---|---|---|
| Failed | 1,165 | 1,139 |
| Valid → never-expanded state | 416 | 439 |
| Valid → expanded off-graph state | 74 | 105 |
| Valid → proof graph | 276 | 278 |
| No progress | 83 | 66 |

- 124/171 (v1) and 126/166 (v2) solved trees are solved at the first expansion. There, every valid partial step is scored like an error in G, and differs only by the λ-weighted −1.

**Why it matters**
- These are false negatives at the most informative nodes.
- Which sibling becomes "the proof" depends on the value model's order and on tie-breaking. That also brings back the value-model circularity you carefully removed from the benchmark.
- For comparison, BFS-Prover's DPO took its negatives from tactics that *failed in Lean*, not from unexplored valid alternatives (from memory; worth checking).

**Recommendation**
- In proof-node groups, treat never-expanded valid siblings as *unknown*: either exclude them from the group, or give them q only.
- Give z = 0 only with evidence: Lean errors, no-progress, verified dead ends, or off-graph subtrees with at least N expansions.
- For training searches, continue a little after the first proof, and at least run the rest of the proving batch (see F8). That produces alternative proofs and siblings that are labelled for real.

### F4. μ-mix value targets: censored negatives, and a q that is not a probability — **critical** (design verified against the code; consequences are judgment)

**What I found**
- The decided design: y = 0.85·z + 0.15·q, with z = 0 for every expanded off-graph node. The only defence against censoring is a weight that grows with subtree size.
- The code (graph_targets.py:29, :41) builds the estimate as `proof_probability = (16 − h)/15` from the recorded scalar `NodeRecord.value_estimate`. h = Σp_k·k + 16·p_dead, so q mixes distance and deadness. Example: a live state with 8.5 expected steps gets q = 0.5 and therefore a 50% "dead" target.
- `bin_target` then *also* spreads the proof probability over step bins around the mean of (distance + h). The dead mass is counted twice.
- DESIGN says the step head is trained only on proof-graph nodes; the code also trains step bins from averaged targets.
- Bin distributions are not stored in the records, so "1 − p_dead" (DESIGN's q) cannot be computed.
- Today about 90% of targets are averaged (v2: 2,560 averaged, 284 proof graph, 266 dead end). Under μ = 0.15 these become ≥ 85% dead. The value model's h would compress toward roughly 13.6–16 for most states.
- A* uses f = 0.25·depth + h, with weights tuned on v0, which never predicts dead. A compressed h changes the effective depth weight, so the v1-vs-v0 A/B would mix in a priority-scale change.

**Why it matters**
- "Not found by a search that stopped at the first proof, under a node expanded k times" is a right-censored observation, not a 0.
- Training it as 0 teaches "states the search abandoned are dead". Because the search only labels what the value model already liked, those errors are never corrected (F9).

**Recommendation**
- Store the full 9-bin distribution per scored node, and compute q = 1 − p_dead from it.
- Model the label as censored: for an off-graph node with n expansions in its subtree and no proof, minimise −log(1 − P(proof within n)). Your original design already had censored "at least n" labels. Alternatively, give concrete z = 0 only to verified dead ends and to large failed subtrees.
- Keep the step head on proof-graph nodes only, as DESIGN says.
- Re-tune depth_weight and D once p_dead becomes non-trivial, or use a priority that does not depend on the readout's scale (for example rank-normalised).

### F5. The trainer's log-probabilities do not match the sampling distribution — **major** (verified)

**What I found**
- Rollouts use temperature 0.8 (`visible_student_v2.toml`).
- `chunk_scores` (policy_trainer.py:146) is a plain `log_softmax` with no 1/T scaling, and nothing under `rl/` mentions temperature.
- `old` (policy_trainer.py:300) is computed from the trainer's own LoRA weights before the phase, so the first step's ratio is exactly 1 whatever the behaviour policy was. Trees that span a publish, data from earlier versions, and vLLM-vs-HF numerics are all invisible to the clip and to "KL to the data policy".
- Forced answers splice in text the policy never sampled (`"\n\n```lean\n"` and a closing fence, prompts.py:189). Those tokens are trained as if they were actions.

**Why it matters**
- The policy-gradient estimator is biased: samples come from π^(1/0.8), but the gradient is taken through π.
- Entropy is reported for a different distribution than the one used for search.
- Recent practice found rollout/trainer mismatch to be a major source of RL instability. Truncated importance sampling with vLLM log-probabilities is the usual fix (from memory: Yao et al. 2025, "off-policy RL training" blog; verl's logits/temperature handling).

**Recommendation**
- Divide logits by the sampling temperature in the trainer, or sample at T = 1.
- Record per-token vLLM log-probabilities (logprobs = 1 is cheap). Use them as the behaviour policy with a truncated importance ratio, and keep the trainer's phase-start log-probabilities for the PPO clip.
- Mask injected forced-answer tokens.

### F6. The control run confounds three changes and is starved of data — **major** (verified)

**What I found**
- Outcome mode scores a partial block 0 even when its prefix reached the proof graph (grpo_groups.py:161), while the dense mode gives it G(prefix). On the v2 trees, 52 of the 278 policy samples that reach the proof graph are partial, and 31 of the 267 proof-path edges are partial.
- The control also switches to sequence-level credit.
- It trains on 145 groups per 244 trees, against 4,279 for the dense arm.

**Why it matters.** A win for the dense arm could come from credit assignment, from how partial blocks are scored, or simply from 30× more updates at the same learning rate. None of these is "the dense signal helps".

**Recommendation.** Run a ladder that changes one thing at a time, matched on the number of optimizer steps, or on summed |advantage| per step:
- (a) outcome-only, sequence credit, with partial blocks scored by their prefix;
- (b) (a) + per-token credit;
- (c) (b) + Lean-verified negatives;
- (d) (c) + local term at an aggregate share of 15%.

If compute allows only two arms, compare (b) and (d).

### F7. The value gate and benchmark: noise, selection on the evaluation split, distribution shift — **major** (verified)

**What I found**
- `GateConfig.min_ranking_gain = 0.01` (graph_trainer.py:61). DESIGN reports ~50 proof-over-expanded pairs per benchmark. At accuracy ≈ 0.7 the standard error is about 0.065, so the gate is close to a coin flip each phase, and repeated application ratchets in lucky models.
- The benchmark trees are built on **miniF2F-valid** (`value_benchmark_search.toml`), the same split as the fixed search evaluation.
- The benchmark search uses cumulative log-probability *including the explanation tokens*: the Lean-STaR failure mode, which your own config comment acknowledges. It also uses a 10 s wall clock instead of heartbeats. Its tree distribution therefore differs from the A* trees the value model works in.
- The benchmark goes stale as the policy changes.

**Recommendation**
- Build the benchmark from a held-out slice of the curriculum pool, with several thousand statements, and refresh it every few phases with the current policy.
- Use the block-only log-probability, or uniform/BFS order, for value-free trees.
- Gate with a paired bootstrap confidence interval (require the lower bound > 0).
- Treat the A/B search on a non-valid set as the real gate, and keep miniF2F-valid for occasional reporting only.

### F8. Groups at the proving expansion are truncated, and not at random — **minor/major** (verified)

**What I found**
- Candidates are executed in descending cumulative log-probability (prompts.py:380), and execution stops at the first proof.
- Proving groups average 9.3 of 16 samples, and 110 groups are incomplete. `PolicyGroup.complete` is computed but never used.
- The proving sample is always the last one executed, the less likely samples are dropped, and the group mean is biased.

**Recommendation.** Execute the rest of the proving batch before stopping: a few Lean calls, and it often yields extra proofs. Failing that, drop incomplete groups.

### F9. Value learning has no exploration: underestimates are never corrected — **major** (judgment, follows from the code)

**What I found**
- Targets exist only for expanded nodes.
- The ranking pair against never-expanded siblings has weight 0.
- Leaf values come from the same model.

**Why it matters.** A state that v1 wrongly rates as hopeless is never expanded, never labelled, and stays hopeless. The "frozen target network" guard does not address selection bias.

**Recommendation**
- Send a fraction of training searches (about 10–20%) through value-free or ε-greedy frontier selection, for example 1 in 5 expansions chosen by policy prior or uniformly from the top-k frontier.
- Train the value model on those trees as well.

### F10. Throughput and the training budget are not planned — **major** (estimate)

**What I found / estimate**
- One hour of search on 4×3090 gives about 400–700 trees (Lean: ~4.3 core-s per expansion × ~33 expansions on average, on ~48 physical cores).
- That is about 15k groups, about 1.1×10⁵ trained sequences of ~600 tokens each (each prompt duplicated 8×), so roughly 7×10⁷ tokens.
- GRPO needs scoring passes (old, and reference when an adapter exists) plus forward and backward. For a 4B LoRA on one 3090 that is perhaps ~1.5–2k tokens/s, i.e. 10+ GPU-hours per search hour.
- `sequences_per_step = 64` × one pass would mean thousands of steps per phase at lr 1e-5.

**Recommendation**
- Fix in advance a per-phase sequence budget, for example a few thousand sequences, sampled with priority to global-signal groups.
- Share the prompt prefix inside a group: one prompt, 8 completions with a block-diagonal attention mask, a 2–4× saving.
- Measure tokens/s for GRPO training before renting for a week.

### F11. Evaluation sensitivity: the likely outcome is "within noise" — **major** (judgment)

**What I found**
- Single runs on 244 problems.
- About 70% of solved problems are solved at the first expansion, so the 64-expansion score mostly measures first-expansion quality plus a long tail.
- The 2B loop showed v0 147 → v3 152 → v9 149.

**Recommendation**
- Primary metric: solve rate on about 1,000–2,000 held-out pool statements at small budgets (1, 4, 16 expansions), with 2–3 seeds.
- Add paired per-problem tests (McNemar or bootstrap).
- Keep per-sample success on the 512 states, but add a variant that counts partial blocks that reach a reference state; the current metric scores PARTIAL as failure while the search keeps the prefix.
- Report miniF2F-valid and -test only at milestones.
- Decide up front what effect size counts as success.

### F12. The pre-GRPO SFT plan: the descriptive-names A/B has a poor cost/benefit — **minor/suggestion** (judgment)

**What I found / judgment**
- **Descriptive names.** Unknown hypothesis names are 3.0% of samples, and the identifier check already catches 71% of them before Lean. The upper bound on the solve-rate effect is therefore small and below noise for a single-run A/B. It needs an LM renaming pipeline, renaming of the evaluation statements, a shift away from about 400k training steps that use `h₀`-style names, and new risks: descriptive names can shadow Mathlib constants (`pos`, `sum`, `prime` …), so the identifier check must handle shadowing. Recommendation: drop or defer it.
- **Mixed step lengths with k ∈ {…, rest}.** This is effectively whole-proof training from any state. Together with F3 it will push toward one-block proofs. That may be good: whole-proof models dominate at matched tokens, and the flattened valid prefix makes your search a truncate-and-resume prover (RMaxTS-like). Recommendation: make it an explicit decision.
- **Interaction with the value model.** Mixed step lengths also change the unit of "remaining steps" relative to v0's labels (one tactic per step from human proofs).
- **Explanations.** Chunked targets need explanations that cover the whole chunk; teacher explanations for single steps will not match.

### F13. Smaller code/design inconsistencies — **minor** (verified)

- `RewardConfig.global_weight = 4.0` and the unclipped `total_reward` are still used for `PolicyGroup.has_mixed_rewards`, `mean_reward` and `prefix_reward`, while the advantages use the clipped λ-mix. These are two reward definitions in one module. It does not change training (`group_weight` uses the advantage's own test), but it is a trap.
- Forced samples can reach about 1,024 + 256 tokens and exceed `max_completion_tokens = 1088`. They are then silently dropped (`sequences_over_length`), so the repetition loops that DESIGN expects RL to dampen are partly never trained.
- Unparsed samples of one expansion are merged under the empty text. That is fine, but they share the "explanation" credit over the whole loop text.
- `phase.toml` still has `[value.source_shares] dead_end = 0.3` and dead-end pairs, which the decided design removes.

## 3. What looks right and should stay

- **Harness:** heartbeat limits as ordinary errors (the `withCurrHeartbeats` elaboration is correct for the Lean 4.9 limitation); flattening with equivalence guards and replay checks; `rename_i`; the identifier check before Lean; verification of both the executed and the plain proof.
- **Proof-graph labelling:** transpositions and sibling-state edges as forward edges; shortest-route remaining steps; no-progress and back-to-ancestor excluded from routes.
- **Benchmark discipline:** recording provenance and refusing value-guided benchmark trees; scoring every model fresh on the same states.
- **Advantages:** mean-centring without std normalization (Dr. GRPO-style); a constant per-step normalizer; dropping groups whose rewards are all identical.
- **Per-token credit:** explanation / valid prefix / failing tactic / masked rest is a sensible decomposition, as long as the rewards feeding it are fixed.
- **Retention:** keeping MATH-500 visible and thinking as a fixed target, with replay.
- **Data infrastructure:** recording full sample texts and tactic spans in tree records.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. What per-phase sequence budget is the trainer actually expected to handle on one 3090, and how will groups be subsampled?
2. Are search and training strictly alternated (pause, train, publish), or can a tree span two policy versions? If it can, what is the plan for behaviour log-probabilities?
3. Why sample at T = 0.8 for RL? Would T = 1 with top-p 1, scored consistently, cost solve rate?
4. In a group at a proof-graph node, should a valid never-expanded sibling really score worse than a no-progress step? It currently scores like an error in G.
5. Is the A* priority formula combining proof probability and steps settled before v1 trains? The v1-vs-v0 A/B depends on it.
6. What is the minimum effect size that would count as "the loop improves its starting policy", and on which set with how many seeds?
7. Is "one-block whole proofs from the current state" a behaviour you want RL to reinforce, or one you want to avoid? F3 and the "rest of proof" chunks both push toward it.
8. Would you accept a cheaper first experiment before building the full loop: 2–3 phases of outcome-only GRPO on global-signal groups only (with T fixed and full proving batches), evaluated at 1/4/16 expansions on a 1,000-statement held-out pool slice? It de-risks the trainer, the signal volume and the statistics.
