# Review D: search-tree GRPO, design and implementation (master @ 14c3164)

Reviewer stance: independent, skeptical. Scope: DESIGN.md "Next phase: search-tree GRPO" to "Known issues", `src/lean_search/{search,rl,value,data_rewrite}`, `experiments/grpo_*.py`, `experiments/configs/grpo/`, `deployment/`. Pure-Python tests run: 204 passed (rl, data_rewrite, loop with fakes). Scripts used for the measurements below are in this folder (`group_stats.py`, `noop_stats.py`, `bf16_merge.py`, `adapter_delta.py`); "old trees" = the 244 visible-student trees (n = 16, no tactic states, no harvesting).

## Executive summary

**Verdict: conditional go.** Nothing found invalidates the plan or forbids renting. But several problems would either waste a large share of the $200 or make the curves hard to interpret, and all of them can be fixed locally (during the half-day node rebuild at the latest). Do not start the GRPO loop itself until the must-fix items below are done. The bring-up, baselines, heartbeat replay and SFT stage can start as planned.

Top 5 issues:

1. **The served policy is not the trained policy (major).** Each version is published by merging the LoRA adapter into bf16 weights (`policy/adapters.py:48-51`, `--dtype bfloat16`). An update below half a bf16 ULP of the weight is rounded away. With W ~ N(0, 0.02) and a per-element delta of 1e-5 (the expected size after one phase at lr 1e-5), 88% of elements stay unchanged and 28% of the update survives. The previous 4B DPO loop is a measured proxy (same lr, rank and batch): after 4 phases, 57–76% of elements of its delta would be unchanged. The planned check (sampler-vs-trainer difference "~0.01–0.05 nats") cannot see the loss, because the loss is the same size as bf16 noise. Effect: early phases barely change what is sampled and evaluated, then updates appear with a lag plus rounding noise. Fix: serve the GRPO adapter unmerged through vLLM LoRA (README: works for Qwen3.5, 0.3 s swap, 5–24% slower), or merge with stochastic rounding. In either case, check after phase 1 how many merged elements differ from the base.
2. **The reward pushes toward short blocks, no-ops and myopic steps (major).** (a) G is binary on the proof graph: closing the proof earns 0.865, one step of progress earns 0.85. The per-tactic credit gives the stop/closing tokens the last tactic's R − b, but gives a continuation only E[R_next] − b. So stopping after any safe step is reinforced more than continuing (worked numbers in §1.2). Nothing rewards getting closer to the proof. (b) A no-op (−0.075) beats a failing attempt (−0.15). On the old trees, 19 of 66 no-progress tactics and 7 of 18 redundant-hypothesis tactics received a *positive* advantage. In the single-step test, where 38% of states have no successful sample, this is systematic. (c) A sibling expanded once (by harvesting or ε) and not proved becomes "off graph" (G = 0, negative advantage). An unexpanded sibling stays neutral. Harvesting thus turns neutral siblings into negatives on one expansion of evidence.
3. **About half of the GPU time is idle (major, budget).** GPU 0 serves nothing while sampling (`loop.toml:68`, no server on GPU 0). GPUs 1–3 serve nothing while GPU 0 does policy training (2 passes plus 2 scoring passes), value training, the gate (scores two adapters every phase), publishing and evaluations. Estimate: a 30-minute sampling phase is followed by 20–35 minutes of single-GPU work, so GPUs are busy about 45–55% of the time. That is roughly $80–110 of the $200. Fixes: a sleeping policy server on GPU 0; keep sampling during training (one version of lag, which the loop already accepts for trees spanning phases); train the value model less often.
4. **The value loop and gate will flip μ by noise (major).** The gate compares point estimates (+0.01 pairwise accuracy on ~50–200 clustered pairs) every phase (`value_every_phases = 1`), so a candidate passes by chance sooner or later. v0 is degenerate on the gate's own metrics: p_dead ≈ 1e-7 everywhere, and pairs are ranked by proof probability first (`benchmark.py:292-295`), so v0's pair accuracy is set by float noise. The first one-phase candidate with any dead mass probably passes and switches μ = 0.15 on for good (`loop.py:564-566`). Two more problems: data from rejected phases is thrown away (`loop.py:259-261` always restarts from the *published* value adapter and trains on one phase's trees), and v0 replay is empty (`loop.toml:82`). The value model can therefore change at random times, and every policy curve after that is confounded.
5. **Evaluations cannot separate policy from value, and are noisy (major).** The probe and miniF2F use whatever value model is being served (`fixed_targets.py:1-5`). The probe's 600 s wall cap (`loop.toml:110`) at 96-way concurrency on 3 serving GPUs probably binds for 16-expansion searches, which makes the budget depend on load and on explanation length. Probe noise is about ±3 pp at 95% (estimate), and there is no replicate baseline. The naming A/B searches both arms with value v0, which was trained on numbered states, so the named arm's states are out of distribution for the value model.

Must-fix before the loop (all local): 1, 2(b) and 2(c) (cheap settings or code changes); 2(a) at least by monitoring block length, better with a progress-aware G; 4 (freeze the value model in the loop or fix the gate); 5 (fixed value model for evaluations, wall cap); orphaned subprocesses (§4.3); replay data (§5.3); a memory budget (§4.2).

## 1. Learning signal

### 1.1 What the per-tactic credit computes (verified against code)

`grpo_groups.py:96-109`, `advantages.py:216-242`, `token_credit.py:105-128`: tactic tokens get R_i − b, explanation and opening-fence tokens get score − b (score = mean of the known R_i), and the closing tokens get R_last − b after a complete block (untrained after a failure or an unknown last tactic). b is the multiplicity-weighted mean score over samples with a known score. Signs are correct: the loss is −min(ratio·A, clip(ratio)·A) (`policy_trainer.py:138-149`). The baseline is the root group's, not the state each tactic acts in. For tactic i > 1, the right baseline would be V(s_{i−1}). Using the root's b inflates later on-graph tactics and deflates later failures. This matters mainly for the stop decision below.

Measured on the old trees (`group_stats.py`): 216 groups. 175 have exactly one policy sample on the proof graph, 18 have none (the sweep made the progress), and 50 are dropped. In 43 of the dropped groups the *only* known sample is a proof sample (all others unknown), so that proof gets no reinforcement at all. Mean sample advantage: +0.52 (299 positive samples) against −0.115 (1,356 negative).

### 1.2 Stop vs continue: a systematic short-block incentive (major)

After a tactic t1 that reaches a proof-graph state, the next token decides between closing the block and writing t2. Gap tokens belong to the next tactic, so the decision token is credited as follows:
- close: R1 − b = 0.85 − b (`advantages.py:224`, `token_credit.py:119-121`);
- continue: R2 − b, with R2 ∈ {0.85 proof graph, 0 off graph, −0.075 no-op, −0.15 fail}, and no gradient at all if t2 is unknown.

Example with b = 0.07 (1 proof, 3 failing, 2 off-graph, 2 unknown samples), P(t2 on graph) = 0.4, P(fail) = 0.4, P(unknown) = 0.2: close gets +0.78, continue gets 0.4·0.78 + 0.4·(−0.22) = +0.22 in expectation. Whenever t2 carries any risk, the policy gradient favours closing. Nothing pulls the other way, because G does not measure progress: reaching a state one step from done is worth the same as any proof-graph state, and closing the proof adds only 0.015. This works against the SFT stage's goal ("choose its own step size") and against the expansion-budgeted fixed targets: more one-tactic blocks means more expansions per proof.

Fix options:
- G = γ^(remaining steps of the reached state), or credit per tactic d(parent) − d(child) on the proof graph. Exact remaining steps already exist (`GraphLabels.remaining_steps`).
- Sum rather than mean in the score.
- At minimum, watch `mean_block_tactics` and the proof length in blocks per phase, and treat a steady fall as a failure signal.

### 1.3 No-ops beat genuine failures (major)

DESIGN says "−0.5 no progress, so a no-op never beats a genuine attempt". That holds only against attempts that make progress. Against a failing attempt the no-op wins (−0.075 vs −0.15). In any group without a positive-G sample, b < −0.075 whenever failures dominate, and then no-op and redundant-`have` samples get positive advantages. Example: 6 failing and 1 no-op gives b = −0.139, so the no-op gets +0.064.

Measured on the old trees (`noop_stats.py`), over trained groups: no_progress 19 positive / 47 negative; redundant_hypothesis 7 positive / 11 negative; off_graph 54 / 51. In the loop this is limited to sweep-only groups (8%), because one proof-graph sample keeps b above −0.075 at n = 8. In the **single-step test** it is the norm: per-sample success is 42%, but 38% of states have no success at all, and those groups train no-ops upward. The docstring's claim (`single_step.py:9-14`) that failing and no-progress samples "go below the baseline of its unknown ones" is wrong: unknown samples are outside the baseline (`advantages.py:229`).

Fix: set `no_progress_local = failure_local` (−1), or drop groups whose maximum G is 0. Either matches "the proof graph decides".

### 1.4 Off-graph after one expansion vs unknown (major)

"Off graph" means expanded and not on a found proof. In solved trees the search stops at the proof, then harvests up to 16 more expansions near done (`search.py:420-437`), and ε draws among the top 8. A sibling expanded *once* by harvesting gets G = 0 (advantage ≈ −b < 0). An unexpanded sibling is neutral. So the label mostly means "not proved within one more expansion", which is myopic and depends on where the search happened to go. The value targets weight this evidence by n/(n+4) (`graph_targets.py:150`). The policy reward does not.

Fix: treat off-graph states with fewer than k expanded nodes in their subtree as unknown (k = 2–4), or scale G's negative weight by the same n/(n+4).

### 1.5 Restricting groups to proof-graph nodes

For the G term, conditioning on "≥ 1 success" removes only groups whose G-advantages are zero anyway, so it is unbiased in the GRPO sense. The cost is data efficiency, not bias. Unsolved trees (about half of all trees at a 30–70% solve rate) and off-graph nodes give no policy gradient. With about 3,000–5,000 expansions per phase and 100–200 trained groups, roughly 2–4% of generated samples are trained on (estimate). Unknown samples are excluded from the baseline rather than given a prior. That biases b slightly downward (unknowns are mostly progress moves) and drops lone-proof groups (20% of groups on the old trees). Option: give unknowns G = p₀, the empirical rate at which never-expanded new states turn out to be on the proof graph (measurable from harvested trees), or μ·q once a value model is trusted.

### 1.6 Closing bonus

0.1 · λ = 0.015 per goal closed, counted for any goal. A flattened `have h : A` adds a goal and earns a bonus when A is proved, so `have`-heavy proofs gain about 0.015 per `have`. Harmless at this size; it does not make closing the main goal special. Minor.

### 1.7 Data per phase and two passes (estimate, unverified)

Throughput is GPU-bound: about 100–200 expansions per minute on 3 GPUs at n = 8 (the old run did 37 per minute at n = 16 on 2 GPUs, but was Lean-bound at 46 cores). Solved trees cost about 3 + 16 harvest expansions and unsolved ones 64, so a 30-minute phase yields about 70–145 trees and 35–70 solved. `min_solved_trees = 100` (`loop.toml:72`) is then rarely reached, and phases will almost always end at the 30-minute cap. At about 2–4 groups per solved tree, minus ~20% dropped, that is about 80–220 trained groups, ~600–1,700 sequences and 10–27 optimizer steps per pass. Two passes with PPO clipping at lr 1e-5 are safe at this size: the ratio stays near 1 and clipping will rarely bind. The risk is the opposite: too little movement per phase, which item 1 then hides further. LoRA lr 1e-5 at rank 64 is at the low end for LoRA RL (LoRA usually needs about 10× the full fine-tuning lr). Sweep it in the single-step test using KL and clip fraction.

## 2. Off-policy behaviour and numerics

- **bf16 merge (major, top 1).** See the summary. Simulation (`bf16_merge.py`): for delta std 3e-6, 1e-5, 3e-5 and 1e-4, the projection kept is 0.09, 0.28, 0.69 and 0.99, and the share of unchanged elements is 0.96, 0.88, 0.68 and 0.32. Measured proxy (`adapter_delta.py`): the previous 4B DPO loop's 4-phase delta (phase6 phase 4 minus its initial SFT adapter) has std 2.2–4.6e-5 per element. Merged into N(0, 0.02) bf16 weights, 57–76% of elements stay unchanged and 57–87% of the projection is kept. The weight distribution is assumed (no 4B base weights locally). The previous expert-iteration loop's null result (147 → 152) may have been partly this; that is speculation. Detection: diff merged against base after phase 1, or compare the sampler's log-probabilities with the trainer with and without the adapter.
- **bf16 logits in the trainer (major, unverified size).** `chunk_scores` runs `lm_head` under autocast, so the logits are bf16 before `.float()` (`policy_trainer.py:160`). The README notes 0.125-nat logit steps at |logit| 16–32, which is 0.156 nats after dividing by T = 0.8. A one-quantum flip moves the ratio by ×1.17 or ×0.86, close to the clip edges (0.8 and 1.28). Old log-probabilities come from different micro-batches (`scoring_micro_batch_tokens`, eval mode, compiled layers) than the training forward, so step 1 may already show ratio noise. Check: the first step's `mean_ratio` should be 1 and `clip_fraction` should be 0. Fix: compute `lm_head` in fp32 per chunk (512 positions × 248k vocab is affordable).
- **Recorded tokens and temperature.** Correct design: the trainer uses the recorded ids, the recorded temperature and top_p 1.0 (`visible_student_v2.toml`), so processed log-probabilities equal the tempered softmax. The prompt is encoded without special tokens; Qwen adds no BOS, so this matches vLLM. Forced answers: the continuation was sampled on a re-tokenized string (prompt + text + fence), and the trainer conditions on the original ids. This is a small mismatch on 2.7% of samples. Minor.
- **Versions straddle phases.** Trees that span a pause hold expansions from older versions, but "old" is the trainer's phase-start weights (`policy_trainer.py:358`) with no importance correction. This is small for solved trees, which are quick, and unsolved trees train no policy. Log the sampler agreement per sampling version. Minor.
- **KL to reference and dual clip.** KL (k3) is measured against the starting weights with coefficient 0. That is fine. There is no dual clip for A < 0 at large ratios, so it matters only if the mismatch in item 1 is large. Minor.
- **Optimizer state.** Adam is re-initialised every phase with 5 warmup steps out of about 20–50. Minor.

## 3. Value model

- **Targets.** z = 0 for every expanded off-graph node in solved and unsolved trees, weighted 0.5·n/(n+4), with 40/60 source shares. At the root of an unsolved tree, z is an unbiased sample of "solved within budget". For interior nodes it is biased low, because they never got the budget. The weighting helps. The likely outcome is that v1 learns problem-level difficulty (p_dead for hard statements) more than sibling ordering. Ranking pairs are sparse (about 50 proof-over-expanded pairs on 244 trees). Harvesting adds many weak pairs (weight 0.1). Whether v1 beats v0 *in search* is open; my prior is "small or no gain within a tree".
- **The gate cannot tell (major, top 4).** Point thresholds with no uncertainty (`graph_trainer.py:609-627`). Pairs are clustered by tree, so the effective N is below the pair count. The gate runs every phase, which is a multiple-comparisons ratchet. v0 is structurally bad at p_dead metrics (ranked by p_dead noise, Brier set by the dead-end share), while the search uses v0 through h = Σ p_k·steps_k. So the gate rewards "has a meaningful p_dead", not "orders the frontier better". Fix: rank benchmark pairs by the quantity the A* priority actually uses, bootstrap over problems and require the lower bound above 0, and gate rarely. Or freeze the value model during the loop and change it only after an offline A/B search (DESIGN step 5).
- **Data accumulation (major).** Every phase continues from the *published* value adapter and trains on that phase's trees only (`loop.py:259-261`, `training_phase.py:115-129`). Data from rejected phases is lost. v0 replay is not wired (`loop.toml:82`, "empty until … checked"). Use a sliding window of the last K phases, keep the last *trained* adapter as the training start, and wire the replay.
- **q is not a probability (major once μ > 0).** `recorded_estimates` maps the scalar h linearly to (16 − h)/15 (`graph_targets.py:28-30, 38-48`), and trees do not record bins. Once μ turns on, q feeds both the targets and the off-graph policy reward with an uncalibrated heuristic. Record p_dead per node, or keep μ = 0.
- **Circularity in loop trees.** Labels in loop trees come from v_k-guided search. ε = 0.1 over the top 8 only partly breaks this. That is acknowledged in the DESIGN and acceptable, provided the gate is fixed.

## 4. Loop systems

### 4.1 Utilization (major, top 3)

See the summary. Phase cadence estimate: about 30 minutes of sampling, then policy training (0.8–1.6M tokens × 2 passes at ~1,900 tokens/s, from the SFT measurement of 3,797 tokens/s on 2 GPUs; 7–14 minutes) plus old and reference scoring (2–4 minutes) plus replay plus value training (6–9k targets on the 2B model, 5–9 minutes) plus gate scoring of 2 adapters (2–3 minutes) plus model loads and torch.compile (2–4 minutes), then about 1–5 minutes of merges and swaps. That is a cycle of about 55–70 minutes with each GPU busy about half of it. Also: the probe runs on 3 GPUs, not 4 (no server on GPU 0), so the "40–60 min" estimate is low by about a third.

### 4.2 Host memory (major, node-only, unverified)

Steady state is 112 REPLs (0.25 GiB after import, restart at 2 GiB private; `repl.py:107`) plus 6 vLLM processes (several GiB of host RAM each), on a node with about 128 GB. During an evaluation, a second process opens 96 (probe) or 120 (miniF2F) more REPLs next to the paused searches' 112 (`rl/README.md`, "next to the paused searches' idle ones"). At about 1 GiB average private memory that is over 200 GiB. Publishing also merges 4B and 2B on the CPU. The OOM killer may pick a vLLM server or the loop process. Fix: write a memory budget, e.g. loop concurrency × p90 REPL memory + evaluation concurrency × p90 + 6 × vLLM RSS + merge ≤ 0.85 × RAM. Shrink the paused pool or restart its REPLs before evaluations, or run evaluations at lower concurrency. Measure in the first hour *with* a probe running.

### 4.3 Orphaned child processes (major, node-only, easy fix)

`run_logged` (`loop_services.py:87-98`) awaits `process.wait()`. SIGTERM (`grpo_loop.sh stop`) cancels the task but leaves the training or evaluation child running. A restart then re-runs the TRAINING stage (`loop.py:375-378`) and starts a second trainer on GPU 0 (CUDA OOM, or two writers to the same checkpoint directory), or a second evaluation pool (RAM). Fix: `try/finally: process.terminate(); await process.wait()` (process group), and refuse to start if a PID file of a live child exists.

### 4.4 Server failure burns the curriculum (major, unverified client behaviour)

On `httpx.HTTPError` the worker abandons the statement, which is a no-op for the curriculum (`loop.py:416-421`, `statement_sources.py:115-117`), and sleeps 5 s. If one policy server dies at night, a third of the requests fail at once. 112 workers each consume a statement every ~5 s, so thousands of statements are used up without trees. Fix: re-queue on transport errors, stop sampling (pause) after N consecutive errors, and alert on the dashboard.

### 4.5 Other items

- **Disk (minor).** Adapters are fp32, 520 MB per policy phase (measured on node_backup) plus the value adapter, kept for every phase, so about 70 GB per 100 phases. Prune old phase adapters (keep every 10th).
- **Phase trigger (minor).** `max_sampling_minutes` with `solved >= 1` (`loop.py:368-369`) allows tiny phases (after a restart, or while the curriculum is in a hard band). Require a minimum number of trained groups instead.
- **No policy gate (minor).** A collapse (entropy, repetition) keeps publishing. Add an automatic stop on held-out per-sample success dropping beyond noise, or on a repetition-loop share.
- **vLLM KV memory (unverified).** The policy servers get 62% of 24 GB, so a 4B (~9 GB weights) leaves about 4–5 GB of KV and recurrent state for about 300 concurrent sequences per server (112 searches × n = 8 / 3). The README already reports preemption for 4B at 256 sequences with more memory. Check preemption counters in the first hour; consider fewer value servers.
- **Restart and publishing logic.** Verified idempotent: publishing is guarded by the trigger, merges by marker, and the stage is persisted. OK.

## 5. Data rewrite and naming A/B

### 5.1 Chunks do not reach the visible mode (major for the SFT stage's purpose)

Chunk examples are built as non-reasoning `replay` examples (`data_rewrite/dataset.py:164-174`: reasoning None, so the non-visible prompt). Reasoning examples are never chunked. The loop's policy runs in visible mode, so mixed step lengths are only taught in the other prompt format, and transfer is uncertain. Check after the fine-tune by measuring the block-length distribution in visible mode on the 512 states. Otherwise build visible chunk examples (a teacher writes explanations for chunks).

### 5.2 Flattening and renaming correctness

The guards are strong. Flattened proofs replay with segment checks and are dropped, not kept nested, when behaviour changes. Renamed proofs must replay to the same states with names replaced, and 10% are verified against the original statement with `rename'`. In the search, every proof is checked against the original. Small residual risks: prose references in explanations that are not exact identifiers (`h0`, "hypothesis 0") stay unrenamed; `obtain`/`intro`-bound names stay numbered, so the named arm mixes conventions. Minor.

### 5.3 Replay data contradicts the SFT stage (major if named wins, else minor)

The GRPO replay is `reasoning/visible_student_v2/train.jsonl` (`loop.toml:79`): v2 data with nested `have` blocks and numbered names. If the starting policy is the rewritten (flat, possibly named) v3, the 0.1-weight replay pulls back toward exactly what the rewrite removed. Point the replay at the v3 build of the winning arm.

### 5.4 A/B fairness and power (major)

The two arms use the same items and differ only in names. That is good. Two problems remain:
- **Value confound.** Both arms run the probe with the loop's value model v0 (`grpo_naming_ab.py:11-15`), which was trained on numbered states, so the named arm's states are out of distribution for it. Run the A/B with log-probability best-first (no value model) or with the value prompts de-renamed.
- **Power.** On about 500 problems at 16 expansions, the run-to-run SD of the solved difference is about 1.5 pp (estimate), so effects under about 4 pp are invisible. Pre-register the rule: adopt named unless its probe result is worse beyond the CI, with the unknown-name rate per policy sample (thousands of samples, high power) and MATH-500 as the deciding measures.

## 6. Evaluation and statistical power

- **Probe.** 501 problems, 16 expansions, a single run. Assuming 10–15% discordance between two runs of the same policy, the SD of the difference is about 1.5 pp, so the 95% minimal detectable effect is about 3 pp and 80% power needs about 4–5 pp (estimate). Plausible gains at this compute are 2–6 pp. Needed:
  - run version 0's probe twice, to measure the noise rather than assume it;
  - report the paired McNemar test against version 0;
  - add per-sample first-expansion success on the probe roots (about 4,000 samples, far tighter) and the anytime curve from the same runs.
- **Wall cap (major).** `max_wall_seconds = 600` for 16 expansions at 96-way concurrency on 3 GPUs. The old runs needed about 35–100 s per expansion under load, so the cap likely binds. Then the probe partly measures load and explanation length, and RL changes the latter. Set the cap to an emergency value (e.g. 3,600 s) and report `wall_clock_budget` stops. Verify on the version-0 probe.
- **Value confound (major).** Probe and miniF2F use the served value model. Once μ or v_k changes, policy and value effects are mixed. Serve v0 under a second model name for evaluations, or report both.
- **Held-out states.** 512 states × 8 samples, per sample; a paired SE of about 1–1.3 pp. This is the most sensitive policy metric; make it the primary endpoint. The single-step test trains on these same 512 states. That is fine as long as no single-step adapter ever feeds the loop's start, but state it explicitly.
- **miniF2F-valid.** 244 problems at 64 expansions with ±5–7 theorems of noise (DESIGN), so likely inconclusive for this loop's effect size. `at_start = false`: request the baseline explicitly before phase 1.
- **Single-step test.** Credit-mode differences are probably below 2 pp, against an SE of about 1.5 pp between arms, so it is likely inconclusive. It also suffers from §1.3. Better use: lr and KL calibration, plus checking that per-sample success on trained states rises.
- **Leakage.** Checked:
  - probe and gate sets are held out from the curriculum by name;
  - pool v3 excludes all traced statements (so no SFT or 512-state overlap) and is near-duplicate-deduplicated (Jaccard ≥ 0.7);
  - miniF2F is decontaminated in the corpora pipeline.

  Not verified: whether any of the 512 states' proofs are in the `4b` proofs the rewrite chunks ("every state of a flat proof is a prompt once"). The eval config says unseen-only with respect to `4b`. Exclude the 512 states' problem names from the rewrite to be sure. A further caveat: the held-out states are never renamed, so a named policy is measured out of distribution there. That is fine within a run, but not comparable with the old baselines.

## 7. Minor items and documentation drift

- DESIGN still describes potential-based shaping h(parent) − h(child) in L. It is not implemented (L = 0 for new states). DESIGN also says "every group has at least one sample on the proof graph", which is false for sweep-only groups (18 of 216 on the old trees), and "a small replay of v0 data (10%)", which is not wired.
- Identifier and forbidden rejections mark tactic 0 as failing even when the offending name is in a later tactic (`tactic_labels.py:96-108`; prefixes are applied for identifier rejections, but forbidden rejections apply nothing). Rare.
- Groups whose largest |advantage| is tiny (bonus differences only) are trained (`ZERO_ADVANTAGE = 1e-12`). None were seen on the old trees. Add a meaningful threshold (e.g. 0.01).
- `probe` difficulty strata use the known proof's tactic count. Goedel proofs are often a single `nlinarith`, so "easy" is not easy for step search. This only affects stratum labels.

## Unverified / estimates

Timing and utilization (§1.7, §4.1), host memory (§4.2), vLLM KV pressure, probe wall-cap binding, bf16 logit ratio noise in practice, the 4B weight distribution in the merge estimate, and probe noise all need first-hour measurements. The checks to add to the first hour:
- (a) the merged-vs-base changed-element count after phase 1;
- (b) step 1 `mean_ratio` and `clip_fraction`;
- (c) version-0 probe twice, with stop reasons;
- (d) RSS of everything during a probe;
- (e) vLLM preemptions.
