## Findings, by severity

### 1. (Medium) The claim keyword is found in trailing comments and nested proofs, so ordinary tactics become claims

**Where.**
- `search/tactic_labels.py:80`: `CLAIM_TACTIC` is now searched anywhere in the text.
- `:123-131`: `claim_made` searches the raw piece text.
- `:296`: it is called on `tactic_texts(edge)`.
- `lean/text.py:217-219`: piece text keeps a trailing `-- …` comment, because `line.rstrip()` keeps it.

**Mechanism.**
- The shape check does not separate a claim from ordinary tactics:
  - the 0-goal shape ("suffices": same context, new target) is the shape of every target-changing tactic (`rw`,
    `simp`, `norm_num`, `field_simp`, `refine ⟨…, ?_⟩`);
  - the +1 shape matches `constructor` (`⊢ A ∧ B` gives `⊢ A`, `⊢ B` in the same context).
- Before this delta, the leading-keyword gate filtered all of these.
- Now any such tactic whose text contains `have`, `obtain`, `suffices` or `replace` anywhere becomes a claim. That
  includes a trailing comment or a `have` inside a nested `by`.
- If its state is unknown or off graph and nothing reachable closes the goal, it is labelled `UNPROVED_CLAIM`.

**Reproduced** (`test_review_v.py`). Each case is labelled `unproved_claim`; the same tactic without the comment or
nested `have` is `unknown`:
- `rw [and_comm] -- we have to swap`;
- `rw [and_comm] -- it suffices to show the swapped form`;
- `constructor -- we have two goals now`;
- `refine ⟨by have h : x + e = 7 := h₀; linear_combination 2 * h₀ - h₁, ?_⟩`.

**Effect.**
- A valid unknown step scores −0.25 instead of −0.075. After progress it costs −2c instead of −c.
- Every later tactic of the block is zeroed.
- The habit monitor's `BARE_HAVE` (`rl/tree_summary.py:27`, `.search` at `:168`) counts `nlinarith [...] -- we have …`
  as a bare-have opening too.
- The SFT corpora keep comments (`strip_comments` is only used in decontamination), so "-- we have …" in blocks is
  plausible. The frequency is unmeasured.

**Fix.**
1. Search the keyword in `lex(text).masked(frozenset({LexicalKind.CODE}))` (`lean/text.py:94,105`), as
   `find_forbidden_tokens` does.
2. Accept it only at bracket depth 0, outside a nested `by`. That means at the start, or after
   `try`/`all_goals`/`any_goals`/`focus`/`·`/`<;>`/`;`.
3. Tie the keyword to the shape:
   - `suffices` for the 0-goal shape;
   - `have`/`haveI`/`letI`/`obtain`/`replace` for +1;
   - `rsuffices` for rotated.
4. Use the same masked search in `is_bare_have` / `is_proof_have`.

Before deploying, measure the share of policy pieces with `--` plus a keyword on a phase's trees.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanics. Frequency unknown.

### 2. (Low-Medium) Nameless sibling matching marks an invented claim proved when a case split has the same branch

**Where.** `search/tactic_labels.py:86-87` (`nameless_key`), `:159-161` (`keys`, every state in the tree) and `:182`.

**Scenario.**
- A bare `have hx : x ≠ 0`, invented and never proved.
- In the same tree, `rcases eq_or_ne x 0 with h | h` followed by `· simp_all` closes the `x = 0` branch. That leaves the
  single-goal state `ctx, h : x ≠ 0 ⊢ T`, which is the claim's continuation up to names.
- The claim is labelled `unknown` (−0.075) instead of `unproved_claim` (−0.25).
- `cases' le_or_lt x 0 with h h` followed by `· nlinarith` does the same for `have hx : 0 < x`, and so does
  `by_cases` + `swap` + closing the negative branch.
- Reproduced: `test_case_split_branch_marks_an_unrelated_bare_claim_proved` and `test_intro_elsewhere_in_tree_proves_claim`.
  Without the split, the claim is unproved (`test_bare_claim_without_case_split_is_unproved`).
- With names this existed only when the names matched (`h` against `hx`). Ignoring names widens it.
- Sign and nonzero guards are exactly the claims models invent most in competition algebra, and case splits on them are
  common in the same trees.

**Fix.** Count a state as a claim's continuation only when it is reached from a claim-like predecessor:
- the state before it (in its edge, or its edge's parent node) has a first goal whose hypothesis types are the
  continuation's minus the added one;
- and that goal's target is either the continuation's target (term-mode `have h : A := p`) or A (the last tactic of a
  flattened proof).

A case split's predecessor has the split hypothesis in its context, so it fails the first condition.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanics. Medium on the frequency.

### 3. (Low-Medium) The replay's continuation check reports FAILS where no inversion exists

**Where.** `rl/reward_replay.py:141-161` (`ContinuationOrdering`); `experiments/reward_replay.py:5` ("Any `FAILS` is a
real inversion inside a group").

**Scenario.**
- The check pairs samples by equal label prefixes, but equal labels can be different states.
- `[PG at 2 steps, U, U]` (complete) scores 0.75. `[PG at 1 step, U, F]` scores 0.85.
- The pair is counted as a violation, although the first sample's continuation (0, then −c) is no worse than the
  error (−c).
- Reproduced:
  - `replay_check.py`: `holds: False`;
  - `replay_graph.py` on a built tree: `valid continuation ≥ error there FAILS (1 of 1 pairs)`, under both rules.
- Groups whose first tactics reach the proof graph at different distances are the normal case, so the real replay
  will print FAILS and hide a real inversion among the false ones.
- Also, `attempt at an invented claim ≥ open bare claim` holds by construction under the new rule (both are exactly
  −0.25). It can only fail under the old rule.

**Fix.**
- Compare the continuation alone: the valid sample's gains from `position` on against the failed sample's gain at
  `position`.
- Or pair only samples whose prefix states match (goal keys or `remaining_steps`), not their labels.
- Mark the tautological check as informational.

**Confidence.** High.

### 4. (Low) A wrapped claim over two or more goals still escapes

**Where.** `search/tactic_labels.py:113-119`. `claim_of` accepts exactly one added goal.

**Scenario.**
- `all_goals have h₂ : x = -4` on two goals adds two claim goals.
- `constructor <;> have h₂ : x = -4` adds three goals to one.
- Both are labelled `unknown` (−0.075) (`test_claim_over_two_goals_escapes`).
- The habit monitor still counts them as bare haves (text-based), and `all_goals` / `constructor` show up in the
  openings by head. So a migration would be visible, though unpenalised.

**Fix.** For a wrapped claim, accept `after` as k interleaved pairs (`⊢ A`, `h : A ⊢ Tᵢ`) for the k goals it ran on, and
key the continuation on the k remaining goals.

**Confidence.** High. The policy is unlikely to find this soon.
