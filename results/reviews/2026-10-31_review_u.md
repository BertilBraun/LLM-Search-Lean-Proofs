## Findings, by severity

### 1. (High) A failed attempt at an invented claim leaves the claim untrained and charges the proof tactic

**Where.** `rl/grpo_groups.py:208-212` and `220-221`. The attempt range is `[claim, failure)`. Every tactic in it gets
gain 0, and the anchor moves to the failing tactic.

**Scenario.**
- `have h₅ : x = 7 := by nlinarith`, an invented false claim, flattened into opener + nlinarith. Labels
  `[unproved_claim, failing]`.
  - The opener's tokens, which carry the invented proposition, get **0**.
  - nlinarith gets the anchor's −0.15 − b₁ (−0.425 in the repro).
- In the same group, `have h₂ : x = -4 := by nlinarith` on a claim a sibling proves is `[unknown, failing]`.
  - Its opener gets −0.075 − b₁ (−0.35); nlinarith gets −c.
  - Score **−0.175**, below the invented claim's **−0.15**.
- Repros:
  - `test_attempt_at_a_provable_claim_scores_below_attempt_at_an_invented_one` (graph level);
  - `test_invented_claim_opener_is_zero_when_its_proof_fails` (opener 0 against −0.35 for a valid step followed by an
    error).

**Effect.**
- 99.8% of open claims go unproved (0.2% proved at v10), so "invented claim, proof fails" is the dominant event once
  the bare form is penalised.
- In that event the claim content gets no gradient. Inventing claims is pushed down only through bare samples.
- The penalty lands on the proof tactics, i.e. nlinarith with hints, which is one of the habits the watch list wants
  back.
- The likely migration is from bare claims to `have <invented> := by nlinarith …` that fails. That migration raises
  `proof_have_share`, which the dashboard will read as recovery.

**Fix (B, keeps the owner's numbers).**
- The claim tactic keeps its R as the anchor, −0.15 − b₁, or −c after progress.
- The tactics after it, the failing one included, get 0.
- The score stays −0.15, but the blame sits on the claim.
- Do this together with finding 2's fix (one change to the attempt range).

**Alternative (A, review T's original).**
- Leaving a claim open costs c once, at the failing tactic or at the closing tokens.
- Bare, failed and partial attempts all score −0.25; a proved claim scores ≥ −0.075.
- `proposed_claim_rule.py` prints both rules on 12 shapes. A also fixes the provable-against-invented order (−0.175 >
  −0.25), but it changes owner decision (2) of 2026-10-31.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanics (reproduced). Medium on the size of the effect.

### 2. (Medium) After an open claim, a valid step or a no-op scores below an error, and appending an error pays

**Where.**
- `rl/grpo_groups.py:218-223`: states after the claim gain 0.
- `243`: the closing costs −c whenever a claim is open and nothing failed.
- `lean/repl.py:539` and `lean/plans.py:360`: a nested proof left open at the block's end is no failure.

**Scenarios** (repros in `test_review_u.py`):

| Block | Score | Against |
|---|---|---|
| `have h : A`, then a valid partial step on A (`[C, U]`) | −0.25 | `[C, F]` −0.15 |
| after progress (`[PG, C, U]`) | 0.85 | `[PG, C, F]` 0.95 |
| `have h : A := by field_simp` left open at the block's end (graph level) | −0.25 | the same `have` with an erroring nlinarith −0.15 |
| `[C, NO_PROGRESS]` or `[C, REDUNDANT]` | −0.35 | `[C, F]` −0.15 |
| two stacked bare claims `[C, C]` | −0.35 | `[C, C, F]` −0.15 |

- The decision tokens are ordered correctly: the valid step gets 0 and then the closing −c, against −0.15 − b₁ for
  the error.
- The explanation (score − b) and the sample ranking are inverted by 0.1–0.2.
- DESIGN.md's decision (1), "a valid continuation never scores below an error at the same place", does not hold
  here.
- `tests/rl/test_reward_ordering.py:50` (`PREFIXES`) has no claim prefix, so the tests miss it.

**Fix.**
- With B: the attempt runs from the claim to the end of the block, failing or not; every later tactic gets 0. The
  closing −c applies only when the claim is the last applied tactic.
  - Then `[C, U]`, `[C, NP]`, `[C, C]` and `[C, F]` all score −0.15, and the bare claim −0.25.
  - Cost: any trailing tactic escapes the extra c, but the claim still scores like a failure, below unknown.
- With A, every open-claim shape is −0.25 (−0.35 with an extra wasted tactic).
- Either way, add `(UNPROVED_CLAIM,)` and `(PROOF_GRAPH, UNPROVED_CLAIM)` to `PREFIXES`.

**Confidence.** High.

### 3. (Medium) A valid continuation into an explored off-graph child still costs about 11 errors

**Where.** `rl/grpo_groups.py:238-241`. A later OFF_GRAPH state gains `R(OFF) − R(reference)`.

**Scenario.**
- `[PG, OFF]` scores −0.075, and its second tactic gets −1.125.
- `[PG, U]` and `[PG, F]` both score 0.95.
- A dead end expanded once counts as OFF.

**Why it matters now.**
- The anchor treats OFF like U (T4's argument: explored and not proved is no better than unseen), but inside a block
  OFF is 1.0 below U.
- The block's own child becomes OFF exactly when the search finds it promising enough to expand ≥ 4 nodes (or once,
  for a dead end).
- Harvest expansions after the first proof add this evidence.
- So "continue past a proof-graph state" carries T1's 0.85 threshold again in proportion to P(child explored off the
  route).
- Review T kept this drop on purpose. It is only a problem if it is frequent.

**Fix.**
- Before deploying, read the replay's `drops` line on phases 1–12: the drop count and its share of the summed |tactic
  advantage|. T's alarm is 10%.
- If it is above that, cap a later OFF state's gain at −c, or require dead ends to have ≥ 2 expansions.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanics; the frequency is unmeasured.

### 6. (Low) Background reference measurement
