## Findings, by severity

### 1. Medium: a rollback beside the held-out tail leaves the collapsed version's samples in the next phase's training data

`rl/loop.py:1085-1092` (`searching` → `roll_back`), with `start_held_out_states` at `rl/loop.py:1672-1674` and
`keep_tree` at `rl/loop.py:880-882`.

**Scenario.** Phase k publishes v(k+1). The held-out job samples every state, and the searches resume on v(k+1) beside
its last Lean checks. Every tree that finishes then goes into phase k+1 (`collecting`). The ~112 searches in flight keep
sampling expansions with v(k+1) until the result arrives. If that result is a collapse, `roll_back` swaps in v(k+2),
which is v_good's policy, around a pause. Phase k+1 then trains from v_good's adapter on a directory that still holds
those trees.

Before this change, the rollback happened while the searches were paused, so v(k+1) never sampled anything. The trainer
does not filter by version. `ExpansionTexts.policy_version` and the sampler log-probabilities feed only the agreement
metrics (`training_phase.py:454`, `policy_trainer.py:335`). The PPO ratio's old log-probabilities are the trainer's own
(`policy_trainer.py:179`). So these samples are plain off-policy data from the policy the health check just rejected,
with no correction.

**Repro.** Phase 2's trees, by the version serving when each finished, come out as `{0: 2, 1: 7, 2: 6}`. Version 1 is
the collapsed one, and 7 of the 15 trees finished on it. The events show `pause, swap ... v2, resume`, so the swap itself
is correctly gated.

**Size live.** The tail lasts minutes ("5 minutes and more" per the README). Up to ~112 of phase k+1's ~600-900 trees
carry v(k+1) expansions, and some finish entirely on v(k+1). The same holds after a hard stop beside the tail: an owner
rollback followed by a restart trains phase k+1 on them too.

**Fix, smallest first:**
- (a) In `roll_back` with `searching`, record the collapsed version, for example in `LoopProgress`. In
  `policy_data.build_policy_data`, or in the persistent job's labelling, drop the groups whose expansion's
  `policy_version` is a collapsed version.
- (b) Or move the phase k+1 trees whose entry or expansions carry v(k+1) out of the phase directory before
  `prepare_training`.

Either way, add a test on this fixture asserting that no trained group of phase 2 was sampled by v1.

**Confidence.** High that it happens (reproduced). Medium on the harm: rollbacks are rare, and the share is about 10-15%
of one phase.

### 2. Low-medium: "solves per tree-hour" is biased against the arm with longer searches, not "alike in both arms"

`rl/budget_signals.py:85` (`wall_seconds`) and `:155-158`, `monitoring/families.py` (`ArmOutcome.wall_seconds`), and
`rl/README.md:738` ("A tree's wall clock includes a pause it waited through, alike in both arms").

**Scenario.** A search straddles a pause with probability roughly proportional to its duration, and each straddler is
charged the whole pause (~25-30 min of training plus wake). The feedback arm's searches run longer by design, so more of
them are in flight at each pause, and each is charged the same pause. With ~112 straddlers per phase against ~140
tree-hours of sampling, pause time is about a quarter of the denominator. The extra pause time grows with the arm's
search length, so it inflates the arm's tree-hours beyond the option's real cost. The held-out early resume shortens the
pause somewhat but does not remove the bias.

The decision metrics (solves per generated token, trainable groups) are unaffected. The tree-hour chart and its
interval are biased.

**Fix.** Charge active time only. `PauseGate.paused_seconds()` is already cumulative: take its difference across each
search in `LeanTreeSearcher.search` and subtract it, or store it as `paused_seconds` on the entry. Alternatively, leave
out trees that straddled a pause from the tree-hour rate. Correct the README sentence.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanism. Low on the size until measured.

### 3. Low: the held-out resume can briefly run ~2x the REPLs the README states

`rl/loop.py:1674` (`resume_searches` beside the tail), `rl/loop.py:826` (the limit gates only new statements), and
`rl/README.md:559-561, 590`.

**Scenario.** The README says the resumed searches "fill what those leave", meaning at most 112 REPLs beside the tail.
In practice:
- At the resume, every search paused in flight continues at once, whatever its worker index. That is
  `searches_running` at the pause, normally about 112.
- `sampling_limit` only stops idle workers from taking new statements.
- When the last state receives its batch, up to ~111 held-out states can still be in Lean.

So up to ~223 Lean processes run at once. With `evaluation.paused_repl_memory_gib = 0`, all ~112 resumed searches also
restart a REPL and replay at the same moment.

Memory still fits at fresh sizes: (112 + ~111) × 0.25-0.5 GiB + 25 GiB vLLM + 14 GiB parked ≈ 95-150 GiB, against
204 GiB. It is close only if the p90 stays near 1 GiB.

The CPU burst lands on the slowest checks the tail holds. Their limits are wall-clock: the 60 s tactic emergency
timeout (`WALL_CLOCK_TIMEOUT`, which counts as an invalid sample) and the 120 s state budget (unjudged samples drop out).
Version 0's reference was measured alone, so contention can lower the held-out valid rate a little. Over ~4,096 samples
the effect is a handful of samples, far from the collapse threshold.

**Fix.** Correct the bound in the README and watch `Host memory as the searches resume beside the held-out states` in
the first pauses. Optionally, count the searches still in flight at the resume against the limit: delay the resume
until the held-out's `searches_in_flight` is at most `concurrency - searches_running`.

**Confidence.** High on the count. Low that it matters at tonight's sizes.

### 4. Low: a stop during the tail loses the tail's sampling seconds, while its trees stay in phase k+1

`rl/loop.py:770`. `run()` saves `sampling_seconds` only in stage `SAMPLING`, and the tail runs in `EVALUATING`. After
the restart, phase k+1 starts at 0 sampling minutes with the tail's trees already in it. The pause clock is off by the
tail's few minutes. The same applies to the pre-existing early resume.

**Fix.** In `run()`'s `finally`, carry `self.sampling_since` over whenever it is set. For example, keep a
`next_phase_sampling_seconds` that `close_phase` seeds. Or accept and document it.

**Confidence.** High. Impact minor.

### 5. Low: `PhaseTiming.searches_paused` reports running searches once the searches resumed

`rl/loop.py:1759`. `close_phase` writes `searches_paused=self.searches_running`. After a resume beside the tail, or the
early resume, this is the number of searches running, not the number paused at the pause.

**Fix.** Record `searches_running` in `run_phase` at the pause, as the log line there already does, and write that
value.

**Confidence.** High. Cosmetic.

## Checked and found correct

- **Pause lock and deadlocks.** `watch` holds `pause_lock` through `run_phase` → `finish_phase`, tail included. So no
  phase pause and no `recover_servers` can start before the health check and the rollback are done.
  - A worker whose servers fail during the tail blocks in `recover_servers` until the phase closes. It is not inside a
    search, so `roll_back`'s `searcher.pause()` (gate idle) does not wait on it.
  - `PauseGate.resume`'s assertion holds on every path: `resume_searches` resumes only when `self.paused`, and
    `pause_searches`/`resume_searches(sampled)` in `roll_back` pair up.
- **Servers never sleep or swap under requests.** `finish_held_out_states` is awaited before `check_health` and
  `roll_back`. In `roll_back`, the swap is gated by `pause_searches` (the repro's event order confirms it). Scoring and
  the evaluations beside sampling run only after `finish_phase`. The durable write is awaited before `EVALUATING`.
- **Versions and value model.** The resumed searches sample v(k+1) (`searcher.serving` in `publish`) with the published
  value model (served at the start of `start_held_out_states`). The held-out job measures `current_version()`, which is
  v(k+1) after the durable record, and its directory matches `held_out_summary_path(published.version)`.
  `HELD_OUT_STATES` is last in `EvaluationKind`, so nothing runs after the resume with another value model.
  `beside_sampling = false` configs still run the probe and miniF2F first.
- **`generation_finished` timing.**
  - The batch is yielded only after `_generate` and `force_answers` complete, and evaluations run with every option off
    (`without_search_options`), so `feedback.enabled` is false there and the first-round batch is the last.
  - Automation batches carry no prompt and do not count.
  - A state with a prompt that is too long, or with no batch at all, counts only at `finished`, which is late rather
    than early.
  - The flag needs every pending state to have started and received its batch.
  - Single-step searches use no value model.
  - A server failure in the job fails the job: the loop stops, as before the change.
- **progress.json.** It is unlinked before the job starts, written atomically by a single writer, and missing means
  false. A held-out run resumed with every tree present reports finished at once and ends at once.
- **Stop and restart in each sub-stage.**
  - Before the signal: the run is cancelled and reruns.
  - After the signal: `mark_evaluated` has not run, so the restart reruns the held-out states on v(k+1) from the trees
    on disk, without an early resume because `paused` is false at the start (the test asserts `[0, 1, 1]`).
  - During the rollback: held-out is already marked and the record written. The restart's `check_health` excludes the
    same phase's earlier record (`earlier = phase < phase`), sets `rolled_back_to` again, and `roll_back` finds the
    trigger recorded and only serves.
  - `searching` is false at a restart, so `pause_searches` cannot hit its `sampling_since` assertion.
  - The `resumed_early` branch never reaches `roll_back`: no held-out ran for that version.
- **Trees per phase.** `collecting` is k+1 from the pause, so the health and budget lines read phase k's trees only.
  Reading `store.entries` in the health thread while workers append is safe (list append, filtered by phase).
- **Phase clock.** `sampling_since` starts at the resume and `close_phase` zeroes the stored seconds, so phase k+1 counts
  the tail. `roll_back`'s pause leaves the swap out. `evaluation_seconds` stops at the resume, as documented.
- **Health fields (item 1).** Feedback edges, expansions and completions are excluded consistently in `tree_summary`,
  `phase_metrics.policy_edges`, `forced_completions` and `data_metrics`. A feedback sample that repeats a first-round
  block is its own `REPEATED` edge flagged `feedback`, so it is excluded too.
- **`keep_config` (item 2).** The parsed comparison is correct, and turning the A/B on does not change an evaluation's
  config (`without_search_options`).
- **Bootstrap (item 3).**
  - It is a percentile bootstrap of a ratio of sums, each arm resampled independently, with a fixed seed.
  - Trees are the right unit: the curriculum feeds each statement once per loop, and arms are assigned per statement.
  - The arm's search config gets the loop's `max_expansions`, because it derives from `search_run_config`.
  - The template turns on no other option, so control and arm differ only in the feedback round.

## Verdict

No blockers for deploying to the live loop tonight. The early resume's pause, swap, version and restart logic holds up
under every scenario I traced. Finding 1 is the one to fix soon: it bites only on a held-out collapse, and costs part of
one phase's training data. Finding 2 should be fixed, or the tree-hour chart treated with caution, before the A/B
decision. The rest are documentation and accounting.
