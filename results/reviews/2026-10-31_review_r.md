## Findings, by severity

### 1. High: the 20-nat cap leaves the KL gradient unbounded below the cap and zero above it

**Where:** `src/lean_search/rl/policy_trainer.py:56` (`MAX_PENALTY_LOG_RATIO = 20.0`), `:202-204` (`kl_penalties`),
`:657`.

**What the cap does.** `kl_estimate(r.clamp(max=20))` clamps inside the exponential. It is not a straight-through
estimator. torch's `clamp` passes no gradient beyond the cap, so:
- a token the reference prefers by more than 20 nats gets **no pull back at all**;
- a token just below the cap gets the largest pull there is.

**The numbers.** This is d loss / d log π per token, from `repro_review_r.py`, at coefficient 0.01 and normaliser
64 × 256:

| r = log π_ref − log π | 5 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 15 | 18 | 20.0 | 20.1 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gradient | 9e-5 | 1.8e-3 | 0.013 | 0.099 | 2.0 | 40 | 296 | **0** | **0** |

For comparison, a policy-loss token with |A| = 0.2 gets 1.2e-5. So:
- one token at r = 12 pulls about 8,000 times harder than a policy token;
- at r = 15, about 160,000 times;
- at r = 20, about 2.4e7 times.

**Why it matters in this loop.** Such tokens are common here.
- `rl/README.md:153` and `:533` record the old k3 step means: 0, or 10^1 to 10^7 (median over a phase's steps), and up
  to 10^9.
- A step mean of 10^1 over about 20k reference tokens needs one token with e^r ≈ 2·10^5, so r ≈ 12. A step mean of
  10^7 needs r ≈ 26.
- So most steps of the drifted phases held at least one token with r between about 12 and 26.
- Version 4 lies inside that drift (phases 3 to 5 had the same reporting).

**Effect.** With `max_gradient_norm = 1.0`, any step holding a token with r of about 12 to 20 will most likely have its
clipped update dominated by that one token's KL term. The 64 sequences' GRPO signal then shrinks by the clip factor.
Tokens past 20 nats, the most drifted ones, are not penalised at all. There is no NaN and no crash: the failure is
silent. The GRPO signal gets diluted, the penalty pulls on a few rare tokens, and the rest of the drift goes
unpenalised.

**How to see it.** It shows up in the step records:
- `gradient_norm` well above the phases 1 to 10 values, on most steps;
- `loss − policy_loss − 0.1 · replay_nll` (the penalty) far above 0.01 × the KL.

**Fix.** Keep k3 below a small cap and continue it linearly above the cap, with the cap's slope. The gradient is then
bounded and never zero. `proposed_penalty.py` checks that the result is continuous and finite up to r = 100.

```python
MAX_PENALTY_LOG_RATIO = 5.0

def kl_penalties(log_probabilities, reference_log_probabilities):
    log_ratio = reference_log_probabilities - log_probabilities
    capped = log_ratio.clamp(max=MAX_PENALTY_LOG_RATIO)
    return kl_estimate(capped) + (math.exp(MAX_PENALTY_LOG_RATIO) - 1) * (log_ratio - capped)
```

- At a cap of 5, the largest per-token pull is about 9e-5, roughly 7 policy tokens.
- At a cap of 10 it is about 0.013, still about 1,000 policy tokens.
- For comparison (from memory, unverified): OpenRLHF clamps the log ratio to ±10, and verl's `low_var_kl` clamps the
  k3 value itself to 10 (r ≈ 2.6).
- Also log the number of tokens past the cap per step, and the penalty term itself, so that this is visible.

**Confidence:**
- High on the mechanism (reproduced).
- Medium on how often it happens at version 4: this is inferred from the k3 step means recorded in the README. I have no
  per-token data on the PC.

### 3. Medium (deployment, not code): the rollback leaves the curriculum band where phase 10 left it

**Where:**
- `experiments/grpo_loop.py` `rollback` only touches `models/`, `rejected_versions` and `health_stop`;
- `curriculum.json` keeps `band_low` (about 6.9).

**Scenario.**
- Version 4 resumes on the band that version 10 reached. The 65% fed from the band are as hard as the statements that
  caused the drift.
- The band only comes down by `band_step` 0.5 per judged cohort. Each cohort is 50 statements, 45 of them finished, and
  the slow ones take up to about 38 min. Getting back to version 4's band therefore takes several cohorts, likely
  hours.

The below-band share softens this but does not undo it.

**Fix (owner's call).** Before `run`, set `band_low` in `curriculum.json` to the band at version 4's phase. Use the
`curriculum` family's `band_low` for that phase, with `generation + 1` and `outcomes: []`.

**Confidence:** high on the behaviour. Whether it is wanted is a design question.

### 4. Low: a test passes for the wrong reason

**Where:** `tests/rl/test_grpo_losses.py:73`
(`test_the_kl_penalty_stays_finite_where_the_reference_prefers_a_token_by_100_nats`).

**The problem.** At r = 100 the gradient is exactly `-0.0`. The test passes because the cap kills the gradient. A
penalty that never pulls such a token back would pass it too.

**Fix.** Assert the direction too, for example `penalty_gradient(-100.0, 0.0) < 0`. That assertion fails today, and it
passes with the fix in finding 1.

**Confidence:** high.

### 5. Low: with a small pool below the band, one statement is re-fed every time it finishes

**Where:** `src/lean_search/expert_iteration/curriculum.py:341-356`.

**Scenario.** There is no minimum spacing or per-phase cap on re-feeds. Once the fresh statements below the band run
out, the least recently searched one comes again as soon as it finishes.

`repro_review_r.py` sets up 112 workers and one verified statement below the band; easy searches are 20 times shorter.
That single statement took 202 of 1,611 feeds: one statement made 12.5% of the trees and their groups.

This cannot happen in the deployment (thousands of fresh verified statements lie below 6.9). It can after a long run,
or with a low band.

**Fix.** Re-feed a statement at most once per phase, or only after k other below-band feeds. Otherwise fall through to
the band.

**Confidence:** high (reproduced). Low impact now.

### 6. Low: an abandoned re-feed drops out of the re-feed pool until a restart

**Confidence:** high.

### 7. Info

- **Easy picks.** Fresh statements below the band are drawn uniformly from all of [min, band_low). That includes
  trivially easy ones, whose groups often carry no signal and are dropped. A window just below the band, for example
  [band_low − 2, band_low), would yield more trainable groups per token. This is a design question.
- **The scan below the band** builds a list over `range(band_start)` on every below-band feed, on the event loop.
  - With `unverified = ""` (loop.toml) `ordered` holds only verified statements, so it is cheap.
  - With a pool of 720k (unverified on) it took 0.15 s per below-band feed.
  - Worth a verified-only sorted list if unverified feeding comes back.
- **Two different KLs.** k3 in the loss, with its gradient taken through log π only, follows the gradient of
  KL(π_ref ‖ π), the forward KL. The health check reads k1, an estimate of KL(π ‖ π_ref). Both are fine. The 0.5 limit
  watches a different quantity than the one the penalty optimises.

## Checked, no issue found

**Curriculum: duplicates while running.**
- Fresh picks are popped from `ordered`.
- Re-feeds are deleted from `searched` until their outcome.
- Searched and started names are never available.
- `test_a_statement_fed_again_is_not_fed_twice_while_it_runs` tests the right thing.

**Curriculum: no starvation or endless loop.**
- With nothing below the band, the credit is spent and the feed falls through to the band.
- The credit is not banked, so it causes no burst later.
- `ordered` empty → None, as before; the existing pool-exhaustion behaviour is unchanged.

**Curriculum: never above the band.**
- Fresh picks take indices below `band_start`.
- Re-feeds need `difficulty < band_low`, using the difficulty at feed time, with the current root values.

**Cohort (judged_share 0.9).**
- Below-band statements never enter `cohort_running`.
- `record_outcome` returns before the cohort logic.
- `state()` ignores statements running below the band.
- The band moves only on band outcomes.

**Restart.**
- `searched` comes from the tree index in append order, which is the finishing order (`TreeStore.add`).
- `mark_searched` deduplicates and keeps the last occurrence; that is about 6,000 inserts.
- Searched statements stay unavailable to the band.
- Searches cut off by the crash left no tree, so they are offered fresh again.
- Held-out trees are not in the loop's tree index, so they cannot be re-fed.

**Concurrency of 112.** `next_statement`, `record_outcome` and `abandon` run on the one asyncio thread, synchronously.
The share holds per feed. Retries bypass the curriculum and keep their `below_band` flag. The share of compute is below
35% because easy searches end sooner, as intended.

**Unverified statements.** `unverified = ""` in loop.toml. Both paths filter on `verified` anyway, with the current
status, so a statement certified later can be re-fed.

**Tree index.** `TreeSummary.below_band` defaults to False, so old entries read as band trees.

**KL direction and normalisation.**
- d k3 / d log π = 1 − e^r, so log π moves toward the reference on both sides.
- The penalty uses Σ weight · k3 / (sequences × 256), the same normaliser as the policy loss, after per-rank
  micro-batch summing.
- Inserted tokens have weight 0. Their capped k3 is finite, so 0 × k3 is not NaN.

**Replay.** `replay_backward` has no penalty and no reference score.

**Reference = SFT adapter after a rollback to version 4.**
- `training_request` passes `config.initial.policy_adapter` (`loop.toml:15`,
  `/workspace/checkpoints/data_rewrite_sft_numbered/adapter`) as `reference_adapter` (`loop.py:520`, `:1374-1377`).
- `initial_adapter` is the published version's adapter, that is, version 4's.
- The `initial_adapter == reference_adapter` shortcut fires only if a rollback targets version 0, and it is correct
  there.
- The swap path (`reference_weights_in_place`) already ran in phases 1 to 10 on 256 sequences.

**Reference scoring on all sequences, 4 ranks.**
- It is the same sharded, gathered, gradient-free pass as the old log-probabilities.
- The log-probabilities kept per token are tens of MB, plus one adapter clone during the swap.
- It costs about one more `scoring` segment per phase, as the README estimates.

**`first_round` and feedback flags.**
- The penalty applies to every trained token, first round and feedback rounds alike.
- The health KL still counts first-round tokens, now all of them scored.
- The feedback A/B has ended (`max_phases = 3`).

**Persistent trainer and restart.**
- `run` reads `phase.toml` at each start and builds every phase's config text from it.
- The resident trainer parses the config text per command.
- `kl_coefficient` and `reference` are existing keys, and `below_band_share` is a new field with a default.
- An interrupted phase is retrained with the new text.
