# Independent review: search-tree GRPO plan for Lean-Proofs

Scope: I read DESIGN.md in full, the literature and error-analysis results, and the RL, search, value, Lean and curriculum code. I also ran read-only analyses. These used the repository's own labelling, target and advantage functions (`label_graph`, `value_targets`, `policy_groups`, `group_advantages`) on the recorded miniF2F-valid trees in the scratchpad. The scripts are in `C:\Users\berti\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\C--Projects-Lean-Proofs\cac68663-9500-4596-8dc6-7bcaea394a3b\scratchpad\review_b\` (`analyse.py`, `analyse2.py`, `solved.py`).

Caveat on the numbers: those trees used n = 16 samples per expansion and v0-guided A*, while the plan uses n = 8, so they indicate scale rather than exact values. Each finding is marked **[verified]** (read in code or computed) or **[judgment]**.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **The local term dominates the gradient, not the "15%" the design intends (critical, verified).** With λ = 0.15 and the phase.toml settings, 85% of the summed |advantage| on the v2 trees comes from groups where only the local term varies. These are almost all in unsolved trees. Only 172 of about 4,500 groups (≈4%) carry any proof-graph signal. λ caps the local share within a group, but there are about 20 times more local-only groups, so in total the local signal drives the policy.
2. **That local signal favours short, "safe" normalisation steps (major, verified).** In unsolved trees, positive-advantage samples average 1.9 block lines and are mostly `norm_num`, `simp_all`, `ring_nf`, `simp`. Negative-advantage samples average 7.4 lines and are mostly `have` blocks. Your own data says long, ambitious visible-reasoning blocks fail more often but solve more problems (171 vs 157). The dense reward pushes against that, and against the planned mixed-step-length SFT.
3. **Children the search never looked at are scored as failures (critical, verified).** In the groups that do carry proof signal, 449 valid samples reached a state with G = 0; 317 of them got a negative advantage. 386 of those states were never expanded (unknown, not failed). The value ranking design gives such siblings weight 0 for exactly this reason, but the policy reward does not. The proof child was v0's top-rated sibling in 71 of 105 cases, so G mostly teaches the policy to imitate v0's ordering.
4. **The current code would train the value model on badly wrong targets (critical until the μ-mix lands, verified).** Averaged targets in unsolved trees have a mean proof probability of 0.83 (median 0.94). Cause: v0 never predicts "dead", and `RemainingStepsReadout` maps h ∈ [1, 3] linearly to p ≈ 0.9. The μ-mix fixes this, but q is still that uncalibrated linear map rather than 1 − p_dead, because tree records keep only the scalar h.
5. **miniF2F-valid cannot detect the expected effect (critical for the stated success criterion, verified).** Across six quite different systems (2B SFT A* up to the visible student v1), 141 problems are always solved, 68 never, and only 35 ever change. The v2 student solves 126 of its 166 at the first expansion. A single-run, dense-vs-control comparison on this set is very unlikely to be significant. The value gate has the same problem: a +0.01 threshold on about 50 pairs, whose standard error is about 0.07.
6. **The outcome-only control is not a matched control (major, verified).** It trains on about 3% of groups (140 vs 4,050 per 244 trees) and also switches from per-token to sequence-level credit. It therefore differs in gradient volume, information and credit assignment at once, and cannot isolate "dense signal helps".
7. **Off-policy and mismatch issues in the trainer (major, verified):**
   - Samples are drawn at T = 0.8, but trainer log-probabilities use T = 1.
   - Completions are re-tokenised from text instead of using the sampled token ids.
   - Forced-answer samples contain text the policy never sampled (`\n\n```lean\n`). Most forced samples exceed `max_completion_tokens = 1088` and are silently dropped, so the design's claim that "RL dampens repetition loops" does not hold with `force_answer_on_truncation = true`.
   - Old log-probabilities are frozen at phase start across what will be hundreds of minibatch steps.
8. **The shaping term is not policy-invariant here (minor in size, verified).** It is a one-step reward clipped inside L, not a telescoping term in a return. In practice it is small: mean |Δh·w| ≈ 0.04 before λ.
9. **Feasibility (judgment).** The training cost is dominated by local-only groups, which are 96% of trained groups, often 64 or more per unsolved tree, and up to 512 with escalation. At n = 8 I estimate about 20–25k sequences per 244 trees, i.e. hours of single-GPU training per phase. That does not fit a 15–30 minute cadence. The prior evidence (the expert-iteration loop gave no gain beyond noise) means the plan needs cheap de-risking experiments before the main run.
10. **Cut the descriptive-hypothesis-name A/B (judgment).** It is high cost for an issue the identifier check already mostly handles (about 3% of samples, 71% now caught before Lean). Spend the budget on evaluation power and a properly matched control.

## 2. Findings

### F1. In aggregate, the local reward dominates the policy gradient (critical, verified)

**What I found**
- `advantages.py` mixes `(1−λ)G + λ·clip(L)` per sample and mean-centres per group without std division. `policy_data.group_weight` gives local-only groups the same weight (`local_only_weight = 1.0` in `experiments/configs/grpo/phase.toml`).
- On `phase5_expansions_visible_student_v2` (244 trees, 166 solved):
  - groups: 4,107 in unsolved trees (3,703 trained), 402 in solved trees (347 trained, 172 with any G difference);
  - Σ|A| is 371 in global-signal groups and 2,083 in local-only groups, so **85% comes from local-only groups**;
  - inside global-signal groups, another 85 of the 371 is local.
- Visible student v1 gives the same picture (85.8%).
- Unsolved trees contribute about 10 times more groups than solved ones (64 expansions each vs a median of 1–4). Escalation stages 192/512 multiply this further. Policy groups have no per-tree cap; value targets do (64).

**Why it matters.** DESIGN.md "Policy update" says "the local influence stays at about 15–20%; the proof graph decides". In aggregate the reverse is true. The loop will mostly optimise "produce a Lean-valid block that changes the state, in hard or dead states of unsolved problems". The dashboard metric `local_advantage_share` would show this, but the design does not anticipate it.

**Recommendation**
- Make λ an aggregate quantity. For example, scale local-only groups so their total |A| is at most about λ/(1−λ) of the global groups' total in each phase, or set `local_only_weight` to about 0.05–0.1.
- Cap policy groups per tree (for example 16) and subsample deep unsolved-tree expansions.
- Alternatively, train local-only groups only on tier-1/2 failures (malformed or ill-typed), as a validity loss with a fixed small weight. That is a clear, bounded objective instead of a general "any new state beats an error" signal.

### F2. Unexpanded siblings are treated as failures in G (critical, verified)

**What I found**
- `grpo_groups.global_value` returns 0 for every non-proof-graph target, including `NEVER_EXPANDED` (DESIGN: "never-expanded children included").
- Global-signal groups (v2): 449 valid G = 0 samples; 317 have negative advantage; 386 reach never-expanded states (280 `new_state` and 93 `sibling_state` to never-expanded nodes).
- The search stops at the first proof (`search.py` `_search` returns on `expansion.proved`), so siblings are rarely resolved.
- The proof-graph child was v0's best-rated sibling in 71 of 105 multi-child proof-graph parents.

**Why it matters**
- This pushes down valid alternative steps that were merely not explored. It amounts to distilling v0's ordering, which you showed is poor on search states (dead/live AUC 0.35–0.55), into the policy.
- It contradicts your own value-ranking design (`proof_over_never_expanded_weight = 0.0`) and benchmark rule (never-expanded = unknown).
- Tree-GRPO (arXiv [2509.21240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21240?context=cs)) reports that intra-tree-only sibling advantages led to training collapse and needed an inter-tree baseline. That is a further warning about relying only on sibling contrasts.

**Recommendation**
- Treat never-expanded valid children as unlabelled: drop them from the advantage, or give them q only once a gated value exists, and exclude them from the baseline.
- Resolve the siblings that matter, i.e. only at the ~4% of proof-graph parents. Either continue the search for +25–50% of its budget after the first proof, or run small sub-searches (4–8 expansions) from each distinct valid sibling (VinePPO-style Monte Carlo, Kazemnejad et al. 2024, from memory). This is cheap because it is so targeted, and it multiplies the number of trusted positive and negative labels.

### F3. The dense signal rewards conservatism, not progress (major, verified plus judgment)

**What I found.** In unsolved trees, the heads of blocks with positive advantage are `norm_num` 3,006, `have` 1,902, `simp_all` 1,679, `ring_nf` 1,352, `simp` 1,242 (mean 1.94 lines). Negative: `have` 13,015, `rw` 3,235, … (mean 7.37 lines).
- The state key includes every hypothesis (`search_error_analysis`), so any trivially true `have` is a `NEW_STATE` with L = 0 versus −1 for an error. That is an easy reward-hacking path.
- Per-token credit helps: a valid prefix gets P and the failing line gets E. But the explanation gets the overall A, so the reasoning that motivates ambitious blocks is penalised.

**Why it matters.** Your biggest measured gain (visible explanations, +14 per expansion) comes from longer, riskier blocks. The planned mixed-step-length SFT aims to let the model pick longer steps, and RL with this reward will push back towards one-line normalisation steps. It also shifts the unit of "step" under the value model and the depth term (see F10).

**Recommendation**
- Track the "trivial `have`" share, the share of new states whose only change is an added hypothesis, and block length per phase.
- Run a local-only ablation to see whether L alone helps.
- Consider making L = 0 for any valid new state in unsolved trees (no evidence either way), keeping only −1 for tier-1/2 errors and −0.5 for no-progress.

### F4. Value targets as currently coded are badly wrong; q is a heuristic, not a probability (critical in code, major in design, verified)

**What I found**
- `graph_targets.RemainingStepsReadout.estimate` maps h linearly onto p between 1 and 16 steps.
- Recorded h on v2 trees is mostly 1–3 (2,286 / 7,994 / 2,236 nodes at h ≈ 1 / 2 / 3; only 32 at 16).
- Averaged targets: unsolved trees, mean p 0.827 (median 0.941, p10 0.48); solved trees, mean 0.747.
- `value_targets` still emits `DEAD_END` and `AVERAGED` sources, and `ranking.py` still builds dead-end pairs (as the brief notes).

**Why it matters**
- Training the value model on current code teaches it that states in unsolved trees are about 83% provable. That is the opposite of the intended fix, and it would also feed G once `use_backed_up_values` turns on.
- Under the μ-mix the damage is bounded (y ≤ 0.15·q), but q stays meaningless until the stored estimate is a real probability.

**Recommendation**
- Do not run value training until the μ-mix lands.
- Record the full 9-bin distribution per node in `TreeRecord` and set q = 1 − p_dead from the search-time model; drop the linear readout.
- Add a unit test asserting that targets in unsolved trees have mean p ≤ μ.

### F5. The value label z is still selected by the value model (major, judgment backed by F2 data)

**What I found.** For off-graph nodes, z = 0 means "this value-guided search did not end up proving through here". Which nodes get expanded, and how deeply, is chosen by the value model being trained. DESIGN.md "Benchmark and gate" correctly identifies this circularity for the benchmark, but it applies to the training targets too. Weighting by subtree size (`size_weight`) only partly mitigates it. In solved trees, off-graph nodes expanded once or twice are mostly "abandoned when a proof was found elsewhere".

**Why it matters.** This is a self-confirming loop: states v0 dislikes get fewer expansions, hence z = 0, hence lower values. It explains why the earlier loop's value training never demonstrably helped.

**Recommendation**
- In solved trees, use only off-graph nodes whose subtree is at least k expanded nodes (for example 4) as negatives.
- Periodically generate value data from log-probability searches or ε-randomised priorities, for example 10–20% of the curriculum searched with priority noise. This provides exploration that does not come from the value model.

### F6. Evaluation cannot answer the success question (critical for the RL goal, verified)

**What I found**
- `solved.py` over six runs: union 176, intersection 141, **35 problems ever change**. The v2 student solves 126 of its 166 at expansion ≤ 1.
- v1 vs v2: 7 vs 2 discordant problems from one run each. Run-to-run sampling noise has never been measured.
- The 512-state per-step metric (`reasoning/reward.py`) counts success only as closing the goal or reaching a reference-proof state. A valid alternative route, or a longer chunk that leaves the reference path, counts as failure, so the metric moves with style, not only ability.

**Why it matters.** "Improves beyond noise, and dense beats the outcome-only control" would need roughly +6 to +8 out of about 100 unsolved miniF2F problems. RL mainly sharpens the distribution (pass@1 rises, pass@k much less: Yue et al. 2025, arXiv 2504.13837, from memory), and a 64 × 8 search behaves like a high-k regime, so the effect shrinks exactly where you measure it.

**Recommendation**
- Primary metric: a fixed held-out probe set of about 1,000 pool-v3 statements in the 30–70% band under the starting policy, never trained on. Search it at a small budget (for example 8 and 16 expansions, n = 8) every few phases and report paired McNemar results against the baseline. At p ≈ 0.5 and n = 1,000 the standard error is about 1.6 points.
- Report solve-rate-vs-expansions curves (1/2/4/8/16/64), where sharpening shows.
- Run the baseline twice to measure run-to-run noise.
- Keep miniF2F-valid as secondary.
- On the 512 states, add a "valid progress" outcome (fully applied and goals changed) next to the reference-match success.

### F7. The outcome-only control confounds three things (major, verified)

**What I found.** `phase_outcome_only.toml`: mode `outcome_only` with `credit = "sequence"`. Only 3.1% of groups have mixed rewards (my count: 140 trained groups vs 4,050 for dense on v2), and `group_weight` drops the rest. Same learning rate, same steps-per-sequence.

**Why it matters.** The dense run gets about 29 times more gradient steps per phase. Any difference, positive or negative, could come from update volume or from per-token credit, not from the dense information.

**Recommendation**
- Match optimiser steps or update norm (for example sample local-only groups so both runs train on similar sequence counts, or normalise by group count).
- Use per-token credit in both arms, or add a 2×2 (credit × reward) on a cheap proxy such as single-step RL on the 512-state distribution.
- Add a plain expert-iteration baseline (NLL on proof-path steps), since that is what the outcome control approximates anyway.

### F8. Policy-gradient plumbing: temperature, tokenisation, forced answers, staleness (major, verified)

- **Temperature.** `visible_student_v2.toml` sets `temperature = 0.8`. `policy_trainer.chunk_scores` uses `log_softmax(lm_head(hidden))`, with no division by T. Ratios, entropy and KL all refer to the T = 1 policy, not the sampler. Fix: divide logits by the rollout temperature in the trainer (the standard practice in mainstream RL frameworks, from memory), or sample at T = 1.
- **Re-tokenisation.** `policy_data.completion_offsets` re-encodes `completion.text`. Non-canonical tokenisations of sampled text would be scored as different token sequences. Fix: store the sampled token ids (and vLLM per-token log-probs) in `SampledCompletion`. That also allows a true behaviour-policy ratio with truncated importance sampling across publishes (training–inference mismatch: see [ms-swift docs](https://swift.readthedocs.io/en/v4.1/Instruction/GRPO/AdvancedResearch/training_inference_mismatch.html) and [arXiv 2605.14220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14220)).
- **Forced answers.** `force_answer_on_truncation = true`. `prompts.forced_sample` splices `explanation.strip() + "\n\n```lean\n" + continuation + "\n```"`, which are tokens the policy did not sample. Truncated samples are 1,024 tokens, and with up to 256 forced tokens most exceed `max_completion_tokens = 1088`, so `fits()` drops them. The rest are trained with the injected tokens. Either way, the DESIGN claim that cut-off samples are L = −1 failures that dampen repetition loops is false for the GRPO search config. Fix: turn forcing off in GRPO searches (truncated means `UNPARSED`, −1), or train forced samples with the forced and injected tokens masked and the truncated explanation penalised.
- **Staleness.** Old log-probs are taken once at phase start (`train_grpo`), with `epochs = 1` and 64 sequences per step. At my estimated volume that is hundreds of steps per phase, so late-phase ratios measure drift from the phase start and clipping will remove most of the gradient. Fix: fewer, larger steps (for example 256–512 sequences per step), or a smaller data volume per phase (F1 helps here). Watch clip fraction per step index, not per phase.
- Minor: a new AdamW optimiser and warmup every phase resets the moment estimates. Consider persisting the optimiser state.

### F9. Shaping justification and reward bookkeeping (minor, verified)

- `shaping()` adds w·(h_parent − h_child) as an immediate reward inside a per-node bandit, and the result is clipped to [−1, 1]. Ng et al.'s invariance needs the potential to telescope through a return. Here it simply adds "prefer children v0 rates closer", a bias of λ·w·Δh. That is small (mean |shaping| 0.038 before λ), but the design text overstates its safety. Either drop it or describe it honestly.
- Two reward definitions coexist. `RewardConfig.global_weight = 4.0` and `total_reward` (β-form) feed `GroupSample.reward`, `PolicyGroup.has_mixed_rewards` and `tree_statistics` (the "93.1% mixed" and "local −45,918 vs global +1,112" figures). Training uses the λ-form in `advantages.py`. Remove the β-form so the dashboard reports what training sees.

### F10. Value and A* units drift with the policy (major, judgment)

- h counts edges (blocks), and A* uses `depth_weight = 0.25` per edge. Mixed-step-length SFT (k ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7, rest}) and RL's push towards one-line blocks (F3) both change what a "step" is.
- v0 was trained on one-tactic human-proof steps. The 8+/dead bins (12/16) and the depth weight were tuned for that regime.

**Recommendation.** After the μ-mix, let A* run mainly on proof probability (log p or p_dead) with remaining steps as a tie-breaker. Re-sweep the depth weight once after the SFT stage, or count depth in tactics.

### F11. The value gate and benchmark are too small to decide anything (major, verified from design numbers plus code)

**What I found.** `GateConfig` thresholds are +0.01 pair accuracy, −0.02 AUC and +0.01 Brier, and DESIGN.md says the headline pair accuracy rests on about 50 pairs. In the row-2 v2 trees there are only 2 proof-vs-dead-end pairs among 4,228 states.

**Recommendation**
- Build the benchmark from log-prob searches on about 1–2k held-out pool statements; this also serves F6.
- Gate on bootstrap confidence intervals of AUC (proof-graph vs expanded-off-graph with subtree ≥ 4) and calibration.
- Make the decisive test the A/B search on the probe set at a small budget, not miniF2F.

### F12. Feasibility and throughput (major, judgment)

- Per 244 trees at n = 8, I estimate about 4k trained groups and 20–25k sequences of about 500 prompt + 250 completion tokens, i.e. ~15–20M tokens. Each sequence needs three forward passes (old, reference, train) and a backward pass.
- Your SFT throughput was about 5k tokens/s per GPU. Training alone would take about 1–2 GPU-hours per 244 trees, against a 15–30 minute cadence.
- Proof-carrying groups (~4%) arrive at roughly a few hundred per hour on 4× 3090.
- Within ~$200 (~250 node-hours), the half-day rebuild, baselines, the SFT A/B pair, value v1 and its A/B, the dense run and the control leave perhaps 2–3 days per RL arm, with one seed each.

**Recommendation.** F1's subsampling cuts training cost about 5–10× and fixes the signal mix. Drop the naming A/B. Budget for two seeds of the baseline evaluation rather than a second SFT variant.

### F13. Cheaper de-risking experiments before the long run (suggestion)

1. **Offline trainer sanity (hours).** Single-step GRPO on unseen states, with reward = closes the goal or reaches the reference state. Check that per-sample success on the 512 states rises over 2–3 phases. This tests the trainer, credit and temperature handling without search.
2. **Overfit test.** Run the loop on a fixed set of about 300 curriculum statements for 3–4 phases. Their solve rate at a fixed budget must rise clearly. If not, the loop is broken.
3. **Label audit on existing trees (done here; extend).** Compute local share, never-expanded negatives and forced-sample drop counts with the n = 8 config on the first new-node trees before any training.
4. **Matched-control pilot.** A short dense vs outcome-only comparison with matched step counts on the probe set at 16 expansions.

### F14. Smaller code and design notes (minor, verified)

- `PolicyGroup.complete` is computed but never used. Proving expansions are truncated, since candidates after the proof are never executed. Harmless, but decide whether truncated groups should train.
- `policy_groups` drops infrastructure failures, which is good. Prompts over 2,048 tokens and over-length completions are dropped after the group mean has been computed. That is fine, but report the drop rate per edge kind; it is the hidden reason forced samples vanish.
- The curriculum (pool v3) excludes statements traced for SFT by exact canonical key only. Near-duplicates (Jaccard ≥ 0.7) of SFT statements can remain. Harmless for training; do not reuse pool v3 as a held-out probe without near-duplicate filtering against the SFT set.
- Transposition reopening (DESIGN.md "Possible later fix") is irrelevant at this scale.

## 3. What looks right and should be kept

- Heartbeat limits instead of wall-clock timeouts. Your analysis showed timeouts were 2.3% of edges but 41.9% of Lean time; this is the single biggest Lean-throughput lever.
- Flattening nested `have` with equivalence guards (`plans.py` `GOAL_LIST_TACTICS`, focus segments), plus the identifier check and `rename_i`.
- The proof graph with transpositions, and no-progress detection via the goal key. The false-merge check was careful.
- Mean-centring without std normalisation (Dr. GRPO style) and dropping groups with identical rewards. Right, once F1 is handled at the aggregate level.
- Per-token credit design (explanation, valid prefix, error line, masked rest), with A = P + E.
- Removing circularity from the value benchmark (log-prob trees, fresh scoring, provenance refusal), the per-tree target caps and the frozen search-time estimates.
- MATH-500 retention tracked per phase, with replay.
- A policy prior weight of 0 with visible reasoning, consistent with Lean-STaR's finding that log-probabilities break with thoughts.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. What fraction of the per-phase gradient should come from unsolved trees? Is "Lean-valid and changes the state" an objective you actually want, given F3?
2. Will you continue the search after the first proof, or run targeted sibling sub-searches, to resolve never-expanded siblings (F2)? What extra budget is acceptable?
3. Can the probe set (about 1k held-out pool statements at a small budget) replace miniF2F-valid as the primary RL metric?
4. Should forced answers be off in GRPO searches? If on, how should the injected tokens be handled?
5. Will the trainer divide logits by the rollout temperature, and will you store sampled token ids and log-probs?
6. In the SFT data rewrite, where do the explanations for multi-tactic chunks come from? The teacher data explains single samples, and the replay data has no explanations.
7. How many sequences and optimiser steps per phase do you expect at n = 8? What clip fraction would make you shorten phases?
8. Is the descriptive-names A/B still worth its share of $200 now that the identifier check catches 71% of unknown-name failures?
